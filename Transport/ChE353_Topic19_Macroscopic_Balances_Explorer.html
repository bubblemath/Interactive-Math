Macroscopic Balances Explorer

A siphon has exactly one thing driving it — the drop from the tank surface to the outlet. This page is about where that driving head actually goes, and why textbook Bernoulli promises you more than twice the flow you will get.

The drop supplies a fixed amount of energy per pound of water. The budget bar divides it up: how much survives as kinetic energy at the outlet — the only part that actually moves water — and how much is burned by fittings and pipe wall. Switch fittings off one at a time and watch the useful share grow.

The momentum balance answers a different question: not how much flows, but how hard the water pushes back on the hardware. A nozzle accelerates the stream, and the two terms in the balance pull in opposite directions — pressure shoves the nozzle forward, the change in momentum tugs it back.

Companion to the ChE 353 Topic 19 study guide. Water at 60 °F throughout: ρ = 62.4 lbm/ft³, μ = 2.76 lbm/hr·ft, gc = 32.174 lbm·ft/lbf·s². Below Re = 2100 the friction factor is the exact laminar result f = 16/Re; above it the page solves the Kármán–Nikuradse smooth-pipe law 1/√f = 4 log₁₀(Re√f) − 0.4, which is the curve the smooth-pipe line of the Moody chart is drawn from. At the lecture's operating point that gives f = 0.0038 against the 0.0039 read off Fig. 6.2-2 by eye, and Q = 47.2 against 46.9 ft³/min — the gap is chart-reading precision, not different physics.
