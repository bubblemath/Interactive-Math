Measuring h

In the lab you know the power and you can read the wall temperature. The fluid temperature you have to work out. These three tabs take you from that bookkeeping to a finished correlation.

Drag the marker along the tube. The shaded area under the flux curve is exactly the energy the fluid has absorbed so far — that is the cumulative quantity. The height of the curve at the marker is the local flux. One picture, both ideas.

Companion to the ChE 319 Lecture 6 study guide. Water at 20 °C throughout (ρ = 998 kg/m³, cp = 4182 J/kg·K, k = 0.598 W/m·K, μ = 1.00×10⁻³ Pa·s). Every tube is treated as perfectly insulated, so all electrical power enters the fluid. In tabs 1 and 2 the wall temperature is generated from an assumed true h so you can check the method recovers it; in a real rig that curve is what your thermocouples measure.
