Catalyst Pellet Explorer

Inside a porous catalyst two clocks are running: how fast reactant can diffuse inward, and how fast it gets consumed on the way. Whichever is slower runs the show — and the Thiele modulus is the single number that says which one that is.

A single cylindrical pore with reaction on its walls. The governing equation is d²CA/dz² = b²CA with a no-flux condition at the dead end — which is the cooling fin from Topic 4, letter for letter. Reactant leaking out through the fin tip is reactant surviving to the end of the pore.

Shrinking the pellet raises η. But each pellet then does less work, because there is less of it. These two facts point in opposite directions and both are true — and which one you care about depends on whether you are buying catalyst or buying a reactor shell.

verdict

Companion to the ChE 319 Topic 22 study guide. First-order kinetics throughout, isothermal pellet, constant effective diffusivity. Defaults are the guide's worked pellet: R = 3 mm, a = 1×10⁷ m⁻¹, k₁″ = 1×10⁻⁶ m/s, DA = 1×10⁻⁶ m²/s, giving Λ = 9.49 and η = 0.283. Careful with the two rate constants: k₁″ in m/s is per unit catalyst area, while k₁″a in s⁻¹ is per unit pellet volume — mixing them up is the commonest way to get Λ wrong by orders of magnitude. The pressure-drop estimate is the Ergun turbulent term's ΔP ∝ 1/dp scaling, shown only to make the trade-off visible; it is not a design calculation.
