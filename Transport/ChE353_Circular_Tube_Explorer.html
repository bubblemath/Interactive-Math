Both problems start from the same general solution, Vz = −Φr²/4μ + C₁ ln r + C₂. In the tube you delete the ln r term; on the rod you must keep it. The difference is simply whether r = 0 is somewhere the fluid actually exists.

Tube — r = 0 is in the fluid, so C₁ = 0

Vz(r) fastest on the axis, zero at the wall

Rod — r = 0 is inside the solid, so C₁ ≠ 0

Vz(r) zero on the rod, fastest at the free surface

Companion to the ChE 353 Topic 11 study guide. The tube model matches the lecture: steady, laminar, fully developed flow of a Newtonian fluid of constant ρ and μ in a long vertical tube with z downward, no-slip at the wall and finite velocity on the axis. The rod problem is the lecture's assigned exercise — a film falling under gravity alone on the outside of a vertical rod, with no-slip on the rod and zero shear at the free surface.
