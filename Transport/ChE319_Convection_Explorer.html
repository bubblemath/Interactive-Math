Three things this lesson asks you to hold at once: where the number h comes from, how to measure a pipe that isn't round, and what the fluid does once it's inside. One tab each.

Pick a fluid and a flow, and watch the chain run: Re decides the regime, the regime decides which correlation applies, and that gives Nu and finally h. The left plot shows the jump that happens when flow turns turbulent.

Reynolds number needs a length scale, and real ducts aren't always round. The rule is four times the flow area over the wetted perimeter. The shaded region below is the flow area; the heavy outline is the wetted perimeter — including any obstruction the fluid touches.
