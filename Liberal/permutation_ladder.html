Lesson 17 · permutations

Order matters, and you stop after r.

A permutation is an ordered arrangement where nothing is used twice. Count it exactly as the counting principle says — n choices for the first place, one fewer for the next — but stop once r places are filled. Three medals means three factors, not eight. That single habit is most of this topic.

✋

Order mattering is what makes it a permutation. Gold to Amara and silver to Ben is a different result from gold to Ben and silver to Amara — the same two people, counted twice, because they finished differently. If swapping two of the chosen items gives a genuinely different outcome, you are counting permutations. If it gives the same outcome, you are not, and this formula is the wrong tool.

Why it matters

Two questions to ask, every time

01

Can anything repeat?

If it can, each slot keeps all n choices and the count is nr. If not, the choices fall away one at a time.

02

Does the order matter?

Medals, rankings and offices care about order. A committee does not, and needs a different count.

03

The formula is just cancelling

n!/(n − r)! is the previous ladder's quotient, and it leaves exactly r factors running down from n.

Start at n, step down one at a time, and stop when r places are full.
