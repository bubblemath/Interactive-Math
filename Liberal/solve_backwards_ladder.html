Lesson 13 · solving for a missing quantity

Three given, one to find.

The formula has four letters. Give any three and the fourth follows — but it follows by rearranging first. Find which letter is missing, get it alone on one side, and only then put the numbers in. Substituting before rearranging is how a division turns into a subtraction and the answer comes out quietly wrong.

✋

To undo a multiplication you divide — you do not take the percent back off. To have $2,000 in two years at 4%, you need 2000 ÷ 1.08 = $1,851.85 today. Taking 8% off $2,000 instead gives $1,840, and that is not the same number: grow $1,840 by 8% and you reach $1,987.20, eleven dollars and eighty cents short. The 8% was a percent of the deposit, not of the target.

Why it matters

Rearranging beats guessing

01

The real questions are backwards

Nobody knows the interest in advance. They know what they borrowed and what they repaid, and want the rate.

02

Dividing is not subtracting a percent

Undoing growth means dividing by the factor. Taking the same percent back off lands somewhere else.

03

The check is free

Run any answer forwards through the formula. If it does not return the figure you were given, it is wrong.

Find the missing letter, get it alone, substitute last. Then check it forwards.
