Lesson 15 · the value of an annuity

A payment every period, not a lump sum.

An annuity is a stream: the same deposit, made again and again at equal intervals. Each one lands at a different moment and so compounds for a different length of time, and the formula adds all of that up at once. The letter P no longer means the money you started with — it means the money you put in each period, and almost every mistake in this lesson follows from forgetting that.

✋

Dividing by r/n is what turns one deposit into a stream. The bracket on top, (1 + r/n)nt − 1, is only the growth a single dollar manages — for $100 a month at 9.5% over 35 years it comes to about 26.4, so leaving out the division gives $2,644. The real answer is $333,946.30, over a hundred times larger. If your annuity answer is the same order of magnitude as one deposit, the division never happened.

Why it matters

Small and regular beats large and late

01

Time does the heavy lifting

The earliest deposits compound the longest, so ten extra years at the start is worth far more than ten extra at the end.

02

Most of the fund is not yours

On a long retirement account the deposits are a small slice. The rest is interest, and interest on interest.

03

It reverses cleanly

Once this formula is solid, rearranging it to find the deposit needed for a target is a single algebraic step.

The bracket for the growth, the division for the stream, then take the deposits back off to see the interest.
