Lesson 6 · Venn diagrams for all, some and no

The picture has to allow every case.

Each of the four quantifier forms has a standard diagram: all is one circle drawn inside another, no is two circles that never touch, and the two some forms are overlapping circles with an ✕ marking where at least one thing has to be. The ✕ is the whole idea — it says something is here, and says nothing about anywhere else.

✋

The last two buttons are the ones worth clicking twice. “Some A are B” is normally drawn with overlapping circles, but it is still true when A sits entirely inside B — every A being a B certainly means at least one is. In the same way, “Some A are not B” is still true when the circles do not touch at all. Everyday English hears “some” as “some but not all,” and the diagrams are where that habit gets caught.

How it works

Why it matters

A picture you can test against

01

It settles disputes about “some”

Draw the nested picture and ask whether the sentence holds. The answer stops depending on how the word feels.

02

Negations become visible

A statement and its negation can never be true of the same picture, and never both false of it either.

03

Arguments come next

Testing whether a conclusion must follow is done by drawing every picture the premises allow, and checking it in each.

Draw what the sentence requires, and nothing more. The ✕ marks what must be there; the rest of the diagram stays open.
