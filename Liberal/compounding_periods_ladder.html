Lesson 14 · compound interest and the compounding period

Divide the rate, multiply the time.

Compound interest pays interest on the interest. To account for that, the formula chops the year into n periods and does two things with that one number: the annual rate is divided by n to get the rate per period, and the years are multiplied by n to count the periods. Both adjustments, every time — doing only one is the error this whole ladder is built around.

✋

Semiannually means twice a year, so n = 2. The word points at the six months between payments, and the 6 is the number that sticks — but n counts the payments, not the months. Quarterly is n = 4 because there are four quarters, not because a quarter is three months. Read every compounding word as “how many times a year”, and the wrong number stops being tempting.

Why it matters

One number, two jobs

01

The exponent is where the growth lives

Compounding beats simple interest because the factor is raised to a power, not multiplied once.

02

More often is always more

Same rate, same term: quarterly beats semiannually, monthly beats quarterly. The gaps shrink as n grows.

03

It sets up everything after

Continuous compounding is what happens to this formula as n runs away, and effective yield is this bracket over one year.

r over n inside the bracket, n times t in the exponent. Both, every time.
