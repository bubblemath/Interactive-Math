Lesson 3 · compound set operations, complements of unions and intersections

Finish the parentheses, then flip.

A prime outside a bracket applies to whatever the bracket produced, not to the letters inside it. So (A ∪ B)′ means build the union first and complement the result. A′ ∪ B means complement A first, then union. Same three symbols, different order, different set — and the order is written right there in the parentheses.

A prime does not distribute the way a minus sign does. It is tempting to turn (A ∪ B)′ into A′ ∪ B′ the way you would turn −(x + y) into −x − y. It does not work: the operation flips too. (A ∪ B)′ is A′ ∩ B′, and (A ∩ B)′ is A′ ∪ B′. Click the first two buttons above and watch the shading stay put; click the third and watch it move.

Why it matters

Two rules that keep coming back

01

De Morgan's laws

The complement of a union is the intersection of the complements, and the other way round. You will meet the same two rules again in logic and in probability.

02

“Not both” and “neither” differ

(A ∩ B)′ says not both. (A ∪ B)′ says neither. In English they blur together; on the diagram they shade completely different areas.

03

The regions do the work

Every expression here is a list of regions. Once you can name the regions, the roster is copying, and the shading is coloring in.
