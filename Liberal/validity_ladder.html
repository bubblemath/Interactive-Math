Lesson 10 · valid arguments and fallacies

Valid means the last column is all true.

Build [P₁ ∧ P₂] → C, fill in its final column, and look. All true means valid: there is no situation where the premises hold and the conclusion fails. A single false row means fallacy, and that one row is a description of exactly how the argument goes wrong.

✋

Valid is not the same as true. A valid argument with a false premise can have a false conclusion, and a fallacy can land on a true conclusion by luck. “If it rains I stay home; I am at home; therefore it is raining” is a fallacy — and on a rainy day its conclusion is perfectly true. Validity is a property of the shape: it asks whether the conclusion would have to follow, not whether it happens to hold today.

Why it matters

Form first, facts second

01

A refutation is one situation

To show an argument fails you describe a single case where the premises hold and the conclusion does not. Nothing more is needed.

02

True conclusions prove nothing

Bad reasoning reaches right answers all the time. That is what makes it hard to notice.

03

Named forms come next

Once you have seen which shapes are valid, you can judge an argument without building anything.

All true, and it is valid. One false row, and that row tells you exactly why not.
