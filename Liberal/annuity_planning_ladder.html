Lesson 15 · planning with an annuity

Start from the goal, work back to the payment.

The real question is rarely “what will this grow to”. It is “what do I have to put in”. Same formula, rearranged: where finding the value divides by r/n, finding the deposit multiplies by it, and the bracket moves from the top to the bottom. Everything else — r/n, nt, the minus one — stays exactly where it was.

✋

Do not divide the goal by the number of deposits. It is the obvious move and it is far too big: $100,000 over 216 months comes to $462.96 a month, when the real answer is $186.44. That division assumes the account pays no interest at all — and in this problem interest supplies nearly sixty percent of the fund. Any answer near goal ÷ nt means the formula never got used.

The naive division is always offered

Conventions. Ordinary rounding to the cent, and percents to the nearest tenth. Your worksheet rounds the college-fund deposit up to a whole dollar; here it is $186.44 rather than $187, which is why the totals on rung 7 differ slightly from the ones in your key.

Why it matters

The question people actually ask

01

Goals come first, payments second

Nobody starts with a monthly figure. They start with a number they need and a date they need it by.

02

Time is worth more than effort

Doubling the term cuts the payment by far more than half, because the early deposits get so much longer to work.
