Lesson 16 · fixed installment loans

Borrowing is saving with the exponent turned round.

A car loan is an annuity seen from the other side: equal payments at equal intervals, except the money is leaving rather than arriving. The formula is the one you already know with two small changes — the exponent goes negative, and the 1 moves to the front of the bracket. Everything else, r over n and n times t, sits exactly where it always did.

✋

A smaller monthly payment can be the more expensive loan. Your worksheet's two car loans make the point better than any warning: $366.19 a month for four years, or $277.89 a month for six. The second is $88 lighter every month and costs $5,008.08 in interest against $2,577.12 — nearly twice as much. Stretching a loan out lowers the payment and raises the price, every time.

03

The denominator is the size check

1 − (1 + r/n)−nt always lands between 0 and 1. If yours does not, the exponent's sign went astray.

Conventions. Ordinary rounding to the cent, and percents to the nearest tenth. Where r/n does not come out as a clean decimal it is left as a division and carried in the calculator rather than rounded partway.

Why it matters

The payment is not the price

Two numbers, not one

03

The same formula buys houses

A mortgage is this calculation with bigger numbers and a longer term. Nothing about the method changes.

Loan times r over n on top, one minus the bracket underneath. Then total the payments and take the loan back off.
