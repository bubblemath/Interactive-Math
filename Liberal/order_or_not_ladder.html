Lesson 18 · permutations and combinations

Swap two of them and see what changed.

Both counts select items from one group with nothing used twice. The only difference is whether the order of what you picked is part of the answer. There is a mechanical test for it: take any two of the chosen items and swap them. If you now have a different outcome, order matters and it is a permutation. If you have the same outcome described differently, it is a combination.

✋

One word can change the answer. Your worksheet's ice cream shop offers the same twelve flavours and the same three scoops twice over. A bowl of three flavours gives 220 possibilities; a stacked cone of three flavours in your preferred order gives 1,320. Nothing changed but the container — and with it, whether the order of the scoops is part of what you ordered.

Why it matters

The wording is the whole question

01

Roles make order matter

Named posts, medal places and rankings all distinguish the positions. A plain committee does not.

02

The gap is enormous

Choosing 5 from 52 gives 2,598,960 hands. Ordering those same 5 gives 120 times as many.

03

Everything else is identical

Same pool, no repeats, same n and r. Getting the classification right is most of getting the answer right.
