Lesson 1 · circle graphs, bar graphs, line graphs

Read the axis, not the picture.

A bar looks twice as tall, so the value must be twice as big. A slice is 22%, so 22 students picked it. Both feel obvious and both are wrong. Every number a graph reports comes from the scale beside it or the total behind it — never from how the shape looks.

✋

The same eight numbers, drawn two ways. Nothing about the data changed between those two buttons — only where the vertical axis begins. Tuition roughly doubled over sixteen years, but on the cropped axis it appears to grow more than thirty times over. Newspapers, ads and annual reports all use this, and it works because people read the picture instead of the scale.

Why it matters

Graphs are arguments, not pictures

01

Someone chose that scale

Where the axis starts, which categories appear, what the total is — all decisions, all made before you ever see the graph.

02

Percents need a whole

40% is a bigger share than 30% and can still be fewer people. Without the total, a percent is not a count.

03

It sets up the rest of the lesson

Rates of change, linear models and projections all start with reading a value off a graph correctly. Errors here travel.

Find the scale, find the total, then answer the question that was asked. The picture is the last thing to trust.
