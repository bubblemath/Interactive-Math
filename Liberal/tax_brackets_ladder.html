Lesson 12 · marginal tax rates and the tax computation

A rate applies to its slice, not to everything.

Being “in the 25% bracket” does not mean paying 25% of your income. It means the top slice of your income is taxed at 25%, while the slices below it are still taxed at 10% and 15%. The tax is built by cutting taxable income at the bracket boundaries, taxing each piece at its own rate, and adding.

✋

Multiplying the whole taxable income by the top rate is the error this lesson exists to stop. A single filer with $28,650 of taxable income is in the 15% bracket, and 15% of $28,650 is $4,298 — but the actual tax is $3,834. The first $9,275 is taxed at 10%, not 15%, and that difference is worth $464. The wrong answer is always too high, and always plausible.

Why it matters

Slices, not a single rate

01

A raise never costs you money

Crossing into a higher bracket taxes only the part above the line. The idea that a raise can leave you worse off comes from this misunderstanding.

02

Marginal and effective differ

The bracket you are in is the rate on your next dollar. The rate you actually pay overall is always lower.

03

Credits are not deductions

A deduction reduces the income being taxed. A credit comes off the tax itself, dollar for dollar, right at the end.

Cut at the boundaries, tax each piece at its own rate, add them, then take the credit off.
