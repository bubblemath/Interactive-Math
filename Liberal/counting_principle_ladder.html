Lesson 17 · the fundamental counting principle

One slot at a time, and multiply.

Draw the choice as a row of empty slots. Work out how many things could go in each one, taking each slot on its own, and multiply the numbers together. That is the whole principle — and almost every wrong answer comes from either adding instead of multiplying, or getting one slot's count wrong because a restriction was missed.

✋

“And” means multiply, not add. A restaurant with 10 appetizers and 15 main courses offers 150 two-course meals, not 25. Adding would answer a different question — how many dishes are on the menu altogether. Multiplying answers the one asked: each of the 10 appetizers can be paired with any of the 15 mains, so each appetizer opens up 15 whole meals of its own.

A note on the digit problems. Where a digit may not repeat, no even-or-odd restriction is added at the same time — that combination needs casework, which belongs after this lesson. Each problem here restricts either repetition or the last digit, never both.

Why it matters

Counting without listing

01

Trees run out fast

Six outfits fit on a page. Six thousand answer sheets do not. The principle is what replaces the drawing.

02

Restrictions live in one slot

“No leading zero” changes one number. Find which slot a restriction touches and only that slot's count moves.

03

Permutations are this with a rule

Once items cannot repeat, each slot has one fewer choice than the last, and the counting principle becomes nPr.

How many for this slot, how many for that one, and multiply what you find.
