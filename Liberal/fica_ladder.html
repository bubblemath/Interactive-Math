Lesson 12 · FICA tax for Social Security and Medicare

One rate below the line, another above it.

FICA is not a stack of brackets climbing upward. It is a single cap at $118,500, with a higher rate below it and a lower rate above. Earn less than the cap and there is one multiplication. Earn more and there are two — and the rate on the part above is the smaller one, which is the opposite of what income tax does.

✋

The second rate applies to the excess only, not to the whole amount. On $200,000 of wages the 1.45% is charged on $81,500 — the part above the cap — not on all $200,000. And the 7.65% below the cap is charged on $118,500 exactly, whatever the income is above it, because that is where the cap bites. Subtract before multiplying, both times.

Why it matters

A cap behaves unlike a bracket

01

The rate falls, not rises

Income tax rates climb with income. FICA's drops at the cap, so a high earner pays a smaller share of each extra dollar.

02

It is charged on earnings

FICA applies to wages and net profits, not to taxable income. Deductions and exemptions do not reduce it.

03

Self-employment doubles it

Someone self-employed pays both halves, which is why the rates are exactly twice the employee's.
