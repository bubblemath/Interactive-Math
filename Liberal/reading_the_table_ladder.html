Lesson 22 · central tendency from a frequency table

A hundred and fifty-one numbers in eleven rows.

A frequency table is a long list folded up. The same four measures still apply, but each one unfolds the table differently: the mean needs every value multiplied by how often it occurred, the median needs you to count along positions until you reach the middle one, the mode is read straight off the frequency column, and the midrange ignores the frequencies entirely. Read the wrong column and you get a number that looks like an answer.

▣

The mode is 7, not 31. In your worksheet's stress table the tallest frequency is 31, and it belongs to the rating 7. The mode is the rating that came up most often, not the count of how often it did. The same trap catches the midrange: it is halfway between the lowest and highest ratings, (0 + 10) ÷ 2 = 5 — not halfway between the lowest and highest frequencies, which would give 16.

How it works

Climb it, or jump to the rung that trips you up

Rung 1 counts how many items the table describes. Rungs 2–4 build the mean: the products column, its total, and the division. Rungs 5–7 are the median, which is the hard one: the position formula, counting along the rows, and reading off the value you land on. Rungs 8–9 are the mode and the midrange, where the usual mistake is reading the wrong column. Rung 10 mixes all four.

Conventions. Answers are rounded to two decimal places where they do not come out exactly. The median position is (n + 1) ÷ 2, counted along the items themselves rather than along the rows. Every table here is already in order from smallest data value to largest, as a frequency table should be.

Why it matters

Two columns, four different readings

01

The mean weighs

A rating given by thirty-one people counts thirty-one times. That is what the products column is for.

02

The median counts

It walks along the items, not the rows. Eleven rows, but a hundred and fifty-one places to count through.

03

The mode and midrange look sideways

One reads the frequency column to find a row; the other ignores the frequencies completely.

The table has two columns, and almost every mistake is reading a value out of the wrong one.

The Table Ladder · n = the total of the frequencies · mean = Σ(x × f) ÷ n · median = the value at position (n + 1) ÷ 2 · mode = the data item with the highest frequency · midrange = (lowest + highest data value) ÷ 2
