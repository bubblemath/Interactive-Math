Lesson 6 · all, some and no — and how to negate them

The opposite of “all” is not “none.”

To deny that all cows are mammals, you do not have to claim that none of them are. One non-mammal cow would be enough. So the negation of All A are B is Some A are not B — and the negations run diagonally across the four forms, never straight down the middle.

✋

There is a test that settles this without any theory. Take “All people are doctors” and “No people are doctors.” Both are false. A statement and its negation can never both be false — exactly one of them has to be true — so those two are not negations of each other. Try the same test on “All people are doctors” and “Some people are not doctors,” and you will find that whichever situation you imagine, exactly one of them holds.

Why it matters

One counterexample is enough

01

That is how claims get disproved

To refute “all,” produce one exception. You never have to show the opposite extreme, and trying to is much harder work.

02

Everyday “some” is narrower

In conversation “some” hints at “not all.” In logic it only means at least one, and that difference changes answers.

03

The pictures come next

Each of the four forms has a Venn diagram, and the diagonal pairs are exactly the ones whose pictures cannot both fit.

All and some-not are negations. Some and no are negations. Nothing pairs straight across.
