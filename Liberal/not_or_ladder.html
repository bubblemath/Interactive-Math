Lesson 20 · probability involving not and or

Add them, then take the overlap off once.

Two rules, and both are about counting each outcome exactly once. Not is a subtraction from 1: every outcome either makes the event happen or it does not, so the two probabilities must add to 1. Or is an addition — but if an outcome satisfies both descriptions it has been counted twice, so the overlap comes off again.

✋

The overlap is not always 1. King or heart subtracts one card, because only the king of hearts is both. Face card or spade subtracts three — the jack, queen and king of spades are all both. Learning "subtract one" as a rule gets the second one wrong by two fifty-seconds. Count the overlap; never assume it.

Why it matters

Count each outcome once

01

The complement is often shorter

"At least one" over four tosses is fifteen cases the long way and one subtraction the short way.

02

Overlap is a question, not a rule

Two descriptions may share nothing, one outcome, three, or one may sit entirely inside the other.

03

It is the set rule from Lesson 3

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B), divided through by n(S). The same counting, now as probability.
