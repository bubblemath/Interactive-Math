Lesson 1 · estimation, place value, rounding

Find the place, then look right exactly once.

Rounding is two moves. Mark the digit in the place you were asked for — that's the rounding digit. Look at the one digit immediately to its right — that's the test digit. Five or more, raise the score; four or less, let it rest. Nothing further right ever gets a vote.

Three slips account for almost every wrong answer. Counting the place from the wrong end — tens and tenths sit on opposite sides of the decimal point. Working right to left, so a 9 at the end quietly bumps everything ahead of it. And forgetting that a whole number keeps its zeros while a decimal drops its tail. Every rung below is built on one of the three.

Why it matters

Estimation is the check on every answer that follows

01

It catches the big errors

A misplaced decimal or a dropped zero turns an answer wrong by a factor of ten. An estimate made first is the only thing that catches it.

02

Place value never goes away

Reading a graph axis, scaling a percent, writing a linear model — all of it is place value wearing a different hat.

03

The rule is short, the habit is not

Everyone can recite "five or more, raise the score." The mistakes come from where you look, not from the rule itself. That takes reps.

Two moves, in order: mark the place, then look right once. Do them in that order and rounding stops being guesswork.
