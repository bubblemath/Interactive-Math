Lesson 3 · union, intersection, complement

Or is both circles. And is the overlap.

Three operations, three regions of the picture. A ∪ B takes everything inside either circle — or in mathematics always includes both. A ∩ B takes only the overlap. A′ takes everything in the universal set that A missed, which means it reaches outside the circles entirely.

The complement is the one that needs the rectangle. Union and intersection can be worked out from the two circles alone, but A′ asks what is in U and not in A — and part of that answer always sits outside both circles, in region IV. This is why a complement is impossible to compute if nobody told you what U is. Being handed A = {2, 5} and asked for A′ is not a hard question; it is an unanswerable one.

Why it matters

Three operations, endlessly recombined

01

Compounds are built from these

(A ∪ B)′ and A′ ∩ B′ are just these three operations stacked. If any one of them is shaky, the compound is guesswork.

02

Everyday “or” is the wrong one

“Coffee or tea” usually means one. In mathematics it always means at least one, so the overlap is always included.

03

Counting overlaps shows up everywhere

Adding n(A) and n(B) counts the shared elements twice. That correction reappears in probability all year.

Union takes everything in either. Intersection takes only what is in both. Complement takes everything else in U — including what is outside both circles.
