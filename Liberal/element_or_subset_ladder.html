Lesson 2 · element of, not an element of, subset, not a subset

Look at what is on the left.

These two symbols are not interchangeable and they are not a matter of taste. ∈ compares a single element with a set. ⊆ compares a set with a set. So before you pick one, ask a single question about the thing on the left of the blank: does it have braces around it? That answer decides the symbol, every time, with no exceptions.

 ? 
✋

One pair of braces changes everything. In the set {a, b, c}, the elements are a, b and c — plain letters, no braces on them. So a ∈ {a, b, c} is true, while {a} ∈ {a, b, c} is false: {a} is a set, and no set appears on that list. Flip to ⊆ and it reverses. {a} ⊆ {a, b, c} is true, and a ⊆ {a, b, c} is not even the right kind of statement, because ⊆ wants a set on its left and it was handed a letter.

Why it matters

The mistake that survives understanding

01

It is not a concept problem

Most students who miss these know perfectly well what a subset is. They lose the points on which symbol goes in the blank.

02

Braces are information

Writing {5} where you meant 5 changes what you said. Notation carries meaning here rather than decorating it.

03

Everything later assumes it

Unions, intersections, complements and Venn diagrams are all written in this notation. A shaky ∈ makes all of them harder than they need to be.

Braces on the left means ⊆. No braces on the left means ∈. Decide that first and the rest is just checking.
