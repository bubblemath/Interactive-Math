Lesson 3 · universal set, Venn diagrams, the four relationships

Four regions, and one home each.

Two overlapping circles inside a rectangle cut the universal set into exactly four pieces: inside A only, inside both, inside B only, and inside neither. Every element of U lands in one of them and never in two. Learn to name the four and most of this lesson turns into looking rather than thinking.

✋

Region IV is the one that disappears. The elements outside both circles are still in the universal set, and forgetting them is the single most expensive habit in this lesson — it silently breaks every complement you will ever compute. When someone reads U off a diagram and lists only what is inside the circles, nothing looks wrong until three questions later.

Why it matters

The picture is a place to compute, not decoration

01

Complements live in region IV

A′ is regions III and IV together. If region IV is missing from your reading, every complement comes out short.

02

Union and intersection become shapes

Once regions have names, A ∪ B is “I, II and III” and A ∩ B is “II.” The arithmetic stops being memorized.

03

Counting gets easier, not harder

Four disjoint piles add without overlap, so counting regions avoids the double-counting that trips up n(A ∪ B).

Name the four regions, put every element in exactly one, and never let region IV slip off the page.
