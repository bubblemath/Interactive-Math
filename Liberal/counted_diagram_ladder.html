Each number counts its own region.

The 55 inside the classical circle is not how many people listened to classical music. It is how many listened to classical and not jazz. The full count is 55 plus the 20 in the overlap. Every question in a survey problem is a list of regions and then an addition — and almost every wrong answer is a missing region rather than a slip in the arithmetic.

The overlap gets counted once, not twice and not zero times. Asked for classical or jazz, the answer is 55 + 20 + 70 = 145 — the 20 belongs, but only once, so adding 75 and 90 to get 165 counts it twice. Asked for classical alone, the answer is 75, not 55. Two opposite errors, both caused by not deciding first which regions the question is asking about.

Why it matters

Regions first, arithmetic second

01

The regions never overlap

That is the whole reason the diagram works. Eight disjoint piles add cleanly, so nothing is ever counted twice by accident.

02

Backwards is the same skill

Given a circle total and one of its pieces, subtraction finds the other. That is how a diagram gets filled in from survey results.

03

It is how the numbers get checked

If the regions do not add to the number surveyed, something has gone wrong upstream, and the diagram will tell you.

Decide which regions the question names. Then add them. In that order, every time.
