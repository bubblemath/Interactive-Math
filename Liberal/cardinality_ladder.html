Lesson 2 · cardinal number, equal sets, equivalent sets, finite and infinite

Equal is which. Equivalent is how many.

Two sets are equal when they hold exactly the same elements. They are equivalent when they merely hold the same number of elements. Those two words sound like synonyms in ordinary English and they are not. One is about identity, the other is about counting, and everything on this page hangs on keeping them apart.

Notice that one combination never appears. Click through all four pairs and you will find sets that are both, sets that are equivalent only, and sets that are neither — but never a pair that is equal without being equivalent. If two sets hold the very same elements, they cannot possibly hold different amounts of them. Equal always drags equivalent along with it. The reverse is not true, and that gap is the whole lesson.

Why it matters

Counting is the easy half

01

The count is only as good as the roster

Almost every wrong cardinal number comes from writing the set out wrong first, not from miscounting what was written.

02

One word does two jobs

“The same” means identical elements in one sentence and identical amounts in the next. Mathematics gives the two meanings separate names for exactly that reason.

03

Subsets are next

Checking whether one set sits inside another means comparing rosters element by element — the same habit this ladder builds.

Write the set out. Count what is actually there. Then decide whether you are being asked about which elements or about how many.
