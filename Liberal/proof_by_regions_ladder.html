Lesson 4 · equality of sets, De Morgan's laws, the distributive laws

Same regions, same set.

There is a way to settle whether two set expressions are equal that never guesses and never needs an example. Work out which regions of a general Venn diagram each one covers. If the two lists match, the sets are equal for every possible A, B and C. If a single region appears in one list and not the other, they are not equal, and that region is the proof.

✋

Checking one example is not a proof. If you pick some sets and both expressions give {2, 5, 9}, all you have shown is that they agree once. The region method is different: a general diagram covers every arrangement the three sets could possibly have, so matching region lists settle the question for all of them at once. That is why your worksheet says this deductively proves the two sets are equal.

Why it matters

A method, not a memory test

01

You can check any claim

Forgotten whether the prime flips the operation? Work out both region lists and find out, instead of guessing.

02

One region is enough to disprove

Showing two expressions are different takes a single region that belongs to one and not the other.

03

The same argument returns in logic

Truth tables do for statements exactly what region lists do for sets: check every case at once.

Two region lists. If they match, the sets are equal always. If they differ anywhere, they are not equal at all.
