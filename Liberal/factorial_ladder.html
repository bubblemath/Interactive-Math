Lesson 17 · factorial notation

Never compute the top.

A quotient of factorials always collapses. Write both out and the whole bottom row appears again inside the top, cancels away, and leaves a short run of the largest numbers — nothing else survives. That is why these can be done without a calculator, and why 100! ÷ 99! is a question you can answer in your head while 100! itself has 158 digits.

✋

9! ÷ 6! is not 3!. Factorials do not subtract like exponents do. 3! is 6; the real answer is 504. What cancels is the shared tail — the 6·5·4·3·2·1 sitting inside both — and what is left is the run from 9 down to 7, which is three factors but nothing like 3!. If the answer to a factorial quotient looks small, the cancelling went wrong.

Why it matters

The shortcut is the point

01

Factorials outrun everything

20! already exceeds two quintillion. Cancelling first is not a convenience, it is the only route.

02

The survivors are a falling run

What is left always starts at the top number and steps down. Knowing how many steps is half the answer.

03

This is the permutation formula

nPr is n!/(n−r)!, which is exactly this cancelling with r factors surviving. Get this and the next ladder is arithmetic.

The shared tail cancels. What is left runs down from the top, one factor for each step of the gap.
