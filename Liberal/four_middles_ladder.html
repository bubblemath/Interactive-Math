Lesson 22 · measures of central tendency

Four answers to one question.

"What is a typical value?" has four standard answers, and on most data sets they are four different numbers. The mean shares the total out evenly. The median stands in the middle once the list is in order. The mode is whatever turns up most. The midrange sits halfway between the smallest and the largest and ignores everything in between. Each is right; each is right about something different.

⇅

Order the list before you look for the middle. Your worksheet's scores are 8, 10, 9, 9, 6, 8, 7, 9. The middle two as written are 9 and 6, which would give 7.5. Put them in order — 6, 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10 — and the middle two are 8 and 9, so the median is 8.5. Sorting is step one, not a tidying-up afterwards.

Why it matters

Which middle you quote is a choice

01

The mean feels every value

Change any one number and the mean moves. That is its strength and, next to an outlier, its weakness.

02

The median barely notices

It cares about position, not size. One enormous value moves it by at most one place along the list.

03

The midrange sees two numbers

Only the smallest and the largest. Everything in between could be rearranged and it would not change.

When someone quotes an average, the first question is which of the four they used.
