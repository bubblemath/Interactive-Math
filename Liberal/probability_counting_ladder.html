Lesson 19 · probability with permutations and combinations

Count the favourable, count the total, divide.

When the sample space is far too large to write out, the fraction does not change — only the way each half is counted. Permutations and combinations arrive to do exactly that. Settle which one the situation needs, use it twice (once for n(S), once for n(E)), and the probability falls out of the division.

✋

The same tool has to count both halves. If n(S) counts arrangements, n(E) must count arrangements too; if n(S) counts selections, so must n(E). Mixing them gives a fraction whose halves are measured in different units, and the answer means nothing. Your worksheet's sales team is the clean case: 12C4 underneath and 8C2 × 4C2 on top — combinations in both places, giving 56/165.
