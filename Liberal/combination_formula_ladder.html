Lesson 18 · the combination formula

Count the arrangements, then divide them out.

The combination formula has two factorials underneath rather than one. The (n − r)! is the same cancelling you did for permutations — it leaves the falling run from n. The extra r! is new, and it is there to undo the orderings: every group of r was counted r! times, once for each way of arranging it, and dividing removes the duplicates.

✋

Choosing 7 of 8 is the same as choosing the 1 to leave out. Your worksheet asks for 8C7, which looks like seven factorials of work and is really 8. Every combination has a cheaper twin: nCr always equals nC(n − r), because naming the group you take also names the group you leave. Before computing anything, check whether the other side is shorter.

Why it matters

Two factorials, two jobs

01

(n − r)! cuts the run short

Exactly as in a permutation. It leaves r factors falling from n, and nothing more.

02

r! removes the duplicates

Every group was counted once per arrangement. Dividing by r! collapses them back to one.

03

The twin saves the work

40C8 needs eight factors. Its twin 40C32 needs thirty-two. Always take the short side.

The falling run on top, r! underneath, and take the shorter twin when you can.
