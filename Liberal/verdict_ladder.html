Lesson 8 · tautology, self-contradiction, or neither

Read the whole column.

A tautology is true in every row. A self-contradiction is false in every row. Anything with a mixture is neither. The verdict is a claim about the entire final column, so a glance at the top row settles nothing — though a single row of the wrong kind can rule a verdict out on its own.

✋

One row can refute a verdict; it can never confirm one. A single false row proves a statement is not a tautology, and a single true row proves it is not a self-contradiction — but neither tells you which of the remaining two verdicts applies. Buttons five and six above are both neither, and one of them opens with a true row while the other opens with a false one. Stopping at the first row would misclassify both.

Why it matters

What a full column can prove

01

A tautology holds no matter what

It is true for every possible assignment of the letters, which is what makes it a law rather than a claim about a situation.

02

Matching columns mean equivalence

Two statements with identical final columns say the same thing. That is how De Morgan's laws are proved here.

03

It is the same argument as before

Checking every row is what checking every region was in the Venn lessons: all cases at once, not one example.

Every row, every time. One row can rule a verdict out, and no row can rule one in.
