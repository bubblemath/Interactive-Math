Lesson 20 · conditional probability

The condition becomes the denominator.

P(A | B) is read "the probability of A given B". Being told that B has happened does not change which outcomes are favourable — it changes which outcomes are still possible. Everything outside B drops out of the sample space, so B's own total takes over as the number you divide by. The numerator is whatever is left of A inside B.

✋

P(A | B) and P(B | A) are different numbers. Your worksheet has both on the same table and the same cell. Of the 27 males, 10 prefer action: 10/27. But of the 19 who prefer action, 10 are male: 10/19. The 10 never moves — the denominator does, and it is the word after "given" that says which total to use.
