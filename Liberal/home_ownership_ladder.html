Lesson 16 · the cost of home ownership

The price is not the loan.

Buying a house produces a chain of different dollar figures, and blurring them is what goes wrong. The price is what the seller asks. Subtract the down payment and you get the mortgage, which is the only number the payment formula ever sees. Points are charged on that mortgage, not on the price. And the total you actually part with is larger than any of them.

✋

Points are a percent of the mortgage, not of the price. On your worksheet's $220,000 house with 20% down, three points cost 3% of the $176,000 borrowed — that is $5,280. Three percent of the $220,000 asking price would be $6,600, which is $1,320 too much. The down payment goes to the seller; the points go to the lender, and a lender charges on what it lends.

Why it matters

Four figures, not one

01

Closing day needs cash

The down payment and the points are due at once, before a single mortgage payment is made.

02

The term is worth more than the rate

Halving the years on the same rate saves more interest than most rate reductions ever will.

03

The sticker is the smallest number

Over thirty years the interest alone can exceed what the house cost. The price is where the arithmetic starts, not where it ends.
