Lesson 20 · probability involving and

Multiply — then ask whether the pool changed.

For one event and then another, the probabilities multiply. That much never varies. What does vary is the second fraction: if the first item went back, the second draw faces the same pool as the first; if it did not, both the favourable count and the total have dropped by one. Every difference between these problems lives in that one fraction.

✋

Only the denominators after the first one move. Your worksheet draws two kings both ways: with replacement it is 4/52 × 4/52 = 1/169, and without it is 4/52 × 3/51 = 1/221. The first fraction is identical in both. Everything that separates the two answers is the single step from 4/52 to 3/51 — one fewer king, one fewer card.

Why it matters

One question decides everything

01

Did it go back?

Replaced means independent and the fractions repeat. Kept out means dependent and every later fraction shrinks.

02

Both numbers drop, not one

Taking a king out leaves 3 kings among 51 cards. Changing only the denominator is the commonest slip.

03

It is conditional probability

3/51 is the chance of a king given that a king has already gone. The next ladder gives it that name.
