Lesson 14 · continuous compounding and the number e

When n runs away, the bracket becomes e.

Compounding more often always pays more, but the gains shrink fast. Push n to infinity and the balance does not run away with it — it settles on a ceiling, and that ceiling is A = Pert. The number e is what (1 + 1/n)n climbs towards as n grows, and continuous compounding is that same limit doing the work.

✋

There is no n in A = Pert. That is the whole point — the compounding periods have already been pushed past counting, so there is nothing left to divide the rate by or multiply the years by. The exponent is simply r times t, with the rate as a decimal and the time in years. And e is raised to that power, not multiplied by it: on $10,000 at 8% for five years, e0.4 gives $14,918.25, while 10,000 × e × 0.4 gives $10,873.13, which is not even close.

Why it matters

A limit you can put a number on

01

e is not an approximation

It is an exact constant, like π. The decimal never terminates and never repeats, which is why the calculator has a button for it.

02

The formula gets simpler, not harder

No n means no division and no extra multiplication. Continuous compounding is the easiest of the three to compute.

03

The same e appears everywhere

Population growth, radioactive decay, cooling. Anything growing in proportion to its own size runs on this constant.

Rate times time, e raised to it, times the principal. No n anywhere.
