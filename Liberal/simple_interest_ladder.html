Lesson 13 · simple interest

Rate as a decimal, time in years.

Simple interest is one multiplication: I = Prt. Nothing about it is difficult once the three numbers are in the right form — and that is where it goes wrong. The rate has to stop being a percent, and the time has to stop being months. Get those two conversions right and the rest is arithmetic.

✋

Four months goes in as a fraction, not as 0.33. On $2,040 at 7.5% the exact working is 2040 × 0.075 × 412, which is $51.00 to the cent. Rounding the time to 0.33 first gives $50.49 — the right method, the wrong answer, and nothing on the page looks amiss. Keep the months over 12 until the very end.

Why it matters

One formula, two conversions

01

The units carry the meaning

A rate is per year. Putting months in as months quietly multiplies the answer by twelve.

02

Rounding early costs money

A third of a year is not 0.33. On a real loan that gap is the difference between the right payment and a wrong one.

03

Everything else builds on it

Future value, present value and solving for a missing rate all start from this same product.

Principal in dollars, rate as a decimal, time in years. Then multiply all three.
