Lesson 11 · sales tax and discounts

Take the percent, then add or subtract.

Tax and discounts are the same two steps with opposite signs. Multiply the original price by the rate as a decimal to get the amount, then add it for tax or subtract it for a discount. Most lost marks here are not arithmetic — they are stopping after step one when the question wanted step two.

✋

The rate has to be a decimal before it multiplies anything. 8.5% of $392 is 0.085 × 392 = $33.32. Using 8.5 instead gives $3,332.00 — a hundred times too much, and an answer that is larger than the television. That is the Ladder 1 error arriving in a place where it costs money, and the size check catches it instantly: tax is a small slice of the price, never a multiple of it.

Why it matters

The same two steps, in both directions

01

Tax and discount are one skill

Identical arithmetic, opposite signs. Learning them apart is learning the same thing twice.

02

The shortcut is how it is really done

× 1.085 for tax, × 0.90 for a tenth off. One multiplication instead of two steps, and it scales to repeated changes.

03

Backwards comes up constantly

Given a total that includes tax, recovering the price means dividing, not subtracting the same percent.

Rate as a decimal, times the original. Then add for tax, subtract for a discount.
