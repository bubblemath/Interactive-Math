Lesson 12 · gross income, adjusted gross income and taxable income

Three subtractions, and each has its own list.

The arithmetic never gets harder than subtraction. What makes this topic difficult is sorting: savings interest is income and goes in, a retirement contribution is an adjustment and comes out, and a mortgage payment is neither — only the interest on it is deductible. Get the lists right and the three lines fill themselves in.

✋

You take the standard deduction or the itemized total — whichever is larger — never both and never the wrong one. This is the only place in the whole calculation where you choose rather than follow a rule, and picking the smaller one leaves no trace: the arithmetic downstream still works, the answer is simply too high. Add the itemized list up first, then compare it with the standard figure and keep the bigger number.

Why it matters

Sorting, then subtracting

01

The lists are the lesson

Every line of a real return is a decision about which pile something belongs in. The arithmetic is the easy half.

02

One wrong pile moves everything

Counting an adjustment as a deduction gives the same taxable income by luck. Counting income as an adjustment does not.

03

Taxable income drives the rest

The bracket you land in, and so the tax you owe, depends entirely on getting this last number right.

Income in, adjustments out, exemptions and deductions out. In that order, every time.
