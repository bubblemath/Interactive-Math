Lesson 11 · decimals, percents and fractions

Two places. The question is which way.

Per cent means per hundred, so every conversion here is a multiply or divide by 100 — which is a decimal point moving two places. Going to a percent it moves right and the digits get bigger. Coming from a percent it moves left and they get smaller. Almost every wrong answer in this topic is two places the wrong way, or one place, or three.

✋

When the digits run out, write zeros. Turning 1.2% into a decimal needs the point to move two places left, and there is only one digit there — so a zero gets written in: 0.012. Turning 100 into a percent needs two places right, and there is nothing to the right of the point at all — so two zeros get written in: 10,000%. The rule never changes; the padding is what makes these look harder than they are.

Every wrong answer is a real slip

The options are what you get from moving one place, three places, or the wrong way — so a miss names the mistake.

Rounding. Percents are given to the hundredth of a percent, so 1/3 becomes 33.33%. Decimals that stop on their own are left exact — 7/8 is 0.875, not 0.88 — and only the ones that run forever get rounded, to the hundredth.

Why it matters

Everything after this multiplies by a decimal

01

Tax and discounts need the decimal

8.5% has to become 0.085 before it multiplies anything. A place out here is a place out in the money.

02

The size check catches most errors

Decide before you convert whether the answer should be bigger or smaller. Then look at what you wrote.

03

Over 100% is normal

Any decimal above 1 gives a percent above 100. Distrusting those answers is a habit worth losing early.

Two places, every time. Right to a percent, left from a percent, and pad with zeros when you run out of digits.
