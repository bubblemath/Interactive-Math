Skill 2.3: Rational Equations; Rate & Work Problems

Goal: solve rational equations by clearing fractions, reject extraneous solutions, and model work and distance-rate-time problems.

Why some answers are fake

The solid curve is the left side of the equation; the dashed curve is the right side. Real solutions are where they cross. Clearing fractions can produce a candidate that sits on an asymptote, where the original equation is undefined. That candidate is extraneous.

      Work-rate builder

      If a job takes t hours, the worker does 1/t of the job each hour. Rates add when working together, and subtract when one works against the other (like a leak).

      Hour by hour

      Sanity check: two workers together must be faster than the faster one alone. Adding the times (6 + 4 = 10 hours) would make them slower!

      Questions to explore

      On the graph, why does the extraneous candidate sit on a dashed line?

      The dashed lines mark x-values that make a denominator 0. Multiplying both sides by the LCD is not allowed at those values (you would be multiplying by 0), so a candidate there is not a real solution.

      In the builder, make both workers take the same time. How long do they take together?

      Half the time: two equal workers do twice as much per hour. For example, 6 and 6 hours give 3 hours.

      Check "against" and make the leak faster than the hose. What happens?

      The pool never fills: the combined rate is negative, so the water level goes down. The builder shows this instead of a time.
