Skill 24.4: Hyperbolas Centered at (h, k)

Goal: graph hyperbolas centered at (h, k) with their asymptotes, write the equation from vertices and foci, complete the square to reach standard form, and tell an ellipse from a hyperbola by the signs of the squared terms.

Move the center to (h, k)

The rectangle, the asymptotes, the vertices, and the foci all move with the center. The asymptotes pass through (h, k): y − k = ±(b/a)(x − h) or ±(a/b).

From general form to standard form

Same steps as for an ellipse. Watch the sign when you factor a negative coefficient out of the y terms (or the x terms).

Which conic is it?

Before any algebra, look at the squared terms of the general form.

Questions to explore

Why does −4y² − 40y become −4(y² + 10y), and what does completing the square do to the left side?

Factoring out −4 flips the sign inside. Adding 25 inside the parentheses adds (−4)(25) = −100 to the left side, so you add −100 to the right side too.

How are the asymptotes of a shifted hyperbola related to those of the centered one?

They have the same slopes and are moved so they pass through the center (h, k) instead of the origin.

How can you tell an ellipse from a hyperbola from the general form?

If the x² and y² terms have the same sign, it is an ellipse (a circle if the coefficients are equal). Opposite signs mean a hyperbola. Only one squared term means a parabola.
