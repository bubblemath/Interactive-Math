Questions to explore

Why does f(x − 2) move the graph right, not left?

The new function needs an input 2 larger to produce the same output. For y = (x − 2)², the output 0 now happens at x = 2 instead of x = 0. Every point slides 2 units right to keep its output.

Move only k. What changes: the domain, the range, or both?

Only the range. Try √x: with k = 3 the range becomes [3, ∞) but the domain stays [0, ∞). Moving only h changes the domain and leaves the range alone.

Pick 1/x and move both sliders. What happens to the asymptotes?

They move with the graph. The vertical asymptote becomes x = h and the horizontal asymptote becomes y = k, so the "center" of the graph is (h, k).

Does the order matter? Shift right 3 then up 2, or up 2 then right 3?

No. A horizontal shift changes only x-coordinates and a vertical shift changes only y-coordinates, so either order lands every point in the same place.

Pick the greatest integer function. Compare h = 1 with k = −1. What do you notice?

They give the same graph: ⟦x − 1⟧ = ⟦x⟧ − 1. Moving a staircase one step right looks exactly like moving it one step down. It is a good reminder to always check with a key point.
