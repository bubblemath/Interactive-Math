Skill 5.1: Parent Functions

Goal: recognize the nine parent functions from their equations and graphs, and know each one's key points, domain, and range.

Parent function gallery

Pick a parent function. Turn on key points, domain, range, and asymptotes to see what makes each graph different.

Questions to explore

Which parents pass through (1, 1)?

All nine. Check: 1, 1², √1, |1|, 1³, ∛1, ⟦1⟧, 1/1, and 1/1² all equal 1. That makes (1, 1) a handy key point to track when a graph is transformed.

Which parents pass through (0, 0)? Which do not, and why?

Seven do. The reciprocal functions 1/x and 1/x² do not, because x = 0 makes the denominator zero. That is also why their domain leaves out 0.

Which parents never go below the x-axis?

x², √x, |x|, and 1/x². Their ranges are [0, ∞) or (0, ∞). Turn on Range to see the shading stay at or above 0.

Compare x³ with ∛x and turn on y = x. What do you notice?

Each graph is the mirror image of the other across the line y = x. They are inverse functions. The same is true for x² (right half only) and √x. You will use this idea in Lesson 6.

Compare 1/x with 1/x². How are they alike? How are they different?

Both have asymptotes x = 0 and y = 0 and the same domain. 1/x has a piece in quadrant III, so its outputs can be negative. 1/x² is always positive, so both pieces are above the x-axis.

Compare x with ⟦x⟧. Where are they equal?

They are equal at every integer: ⟦2⟧ = 2, ⟦−3⟧ = −3. Between integers the step function stays flat while the line keeps rising.
