Skill 22.4: Identities and Multiple Angles

Goal: use an identity to rewrite a trig equation with one function before solving, and solve equations such as cos(2x) = 1/2 by finding every value of u = 2x on the longer interval.

Multiple angles: stretch the interval

To solve f(kx) = c on [0, 2π), let u = kx. As x runs over [0, 2π), u runs over [0, 2kπ): find every u there, then divide by k.

Rewrite with one function

When an equation mixes functions or angles, an identity can turn it into one function of one angle. Then solve as before.

Questions to explore

Why does cos(2x) = 1/2 have four solutions on [0, 2π)?

cos(2x) completes two full cycles on [0, 2π), and each cycle crosses the line y = 1/2 twice.

What goes wrong if you solve 2x = π/3 and 2x = 5π/3 only?

You find x = π/6 and 5π/6 and miss 7π/6 and 11π/6, which come from 2x = 7π/3 and 11π/3 in the second turn.

For cos(2θ) = cos θ, which form of cos(2θ) is best?

2cos²θ − 1, because it leaves only cosine. The form 1 − 2sin²θ would mix sine and cosine.
