Skill 16.4: Graphing with Key Points

Goal: graph y = A sin(Bx − C) + D or y = A cos(Bx − C) + D over one period by finding five key points: start at the phase shift, step by a quarter period, and scale the basic pattern.

Build the graph step by step

Every sine or cosine graph comes from the same five-point pattern. Pick a function and step through the method.

    Questions to explore

    Why do the key points always split the period into four equal parts?

    The basic graphs have their key points at 0, π/2, π, 3π/2, 2π: quarter turns. Stretching and shifting keeps them equally spaced, P/4 apart.

    Where do you start when A is negative?

    At the same x-value, the phase shift. Only the heights flip: a negative sine goes down first, and a negative cosine starts at a minimum.

    Try the preset y = −2cos((πx)/2 + π) + 3. Why does the cycle start at a negative x?

    The phase shift is C/B = −π ÷ π/2 = −2, a shift left. The five x-values are −2, −1, 0, 1, 2.
