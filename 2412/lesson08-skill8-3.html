Skill 8.3: Complex Zeros & Building Polynomials

Goal: use the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra to count zeros, apply the Conjugate Zeros Theorem, and build a polynomial with given zeros.

Build a polynomial from its zeros

Add zeros one at a time. A polynomial with real coefficients that has a complex zero a + bi must also have a − bi, so the builder adds the conjugate for you (dashed). Watch the degree: it always equals the number of zeros.

    Only real zeros are x-intercepts. Complex zeros are still zeros, but they do not show on the graph.

    Questions to explore

    Add 1 and 5i. Why is the degree 3, not 2?

    The conjugate −5i comes along. Three zeros give (x − 1)(x − 5i)(x + 5i) = (x − 1)(x² + 25), degree 3.

    Can a cubic with real coefficients have no real zeros?

    No. Complex zeros come in pairs, so a cubic has 0 or 2 non-real zeros, leaving at least one real zero. Its graph must cross the x-axis.

    Why multiply conjugate factors together first?

    (x − (a + bi))(x − (a − bi)) = x² − 2ax + a² + b² has real coefficients. The i's cancel before they can spread through the rest of the work.
