Skill 1.3: Multiplying & Dividing Rational Expressions

Goal: simplify, multiply, and divide rational expressions by factoring and cancelling common factors (never terms), and state the values x cannot be.

Cancel machine

Work through the four steps. In step 3, click a factor on top and a matching factor on the bottom to cancel them. Only identical factors cancel.

Values x cannot be

A denominator can never be 0. When dividing, the second fraction's numerator ends up on the bottom too, so its zeros are also excluded.

Why keep them after cancelling? The simplified answer has to be equal to the original for every allowed x. The original is undefined at these values, so the answer must say so too. (This is where "holes" in graphs come from, in Lesson 9.)

Factor or term?

Can anything cancel? Decide, then check.

Questions to explore

Why can't you cancel the x in (x + 4)/(x + 7)? Test with x = 1.

(1 + 4)/(1 + 7) = 5/8. Crossing out the x's would leave 4/7, a different number. The x is a term (it is added), not a factor (multiplied by everything).

In a division problem, which fraction gets flipped?

Only the second one, the divisor: (A/B) ÷ (C/D) = (A/B)·(D/C). Flipping the first one gives the reciprocal of the right answer.

Can everything cancel? What is left then?

Yes. When every factor cancels, the answer is 1 (still with its excluded values). Try the Divide problems until you see one.
