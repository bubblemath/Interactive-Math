Skill 2.1: Linear Equations & Linear Models

Goal: solve linear equations and use the five-step process to model mixture, cost, and geometry problems.

Step checker

Solve the equation one line at a time. Type each new line as an equation. The checker tells you whether your line still has the same solution, so you can find the exact step where a mistake happens.

    Preview:

    What keeps an equation equivalent?

    • Simplify one side (distribute, combine like terms).
    • Add or subtract the same thing on both sides.
    • Multiply or divide both sides by the same nonzero number.
    Most common slip: distributing a negative. −5(x + 6) = −5x − 30, not −5x + 6.

    You are done when your line reads x = a number.

      Questions to explore

      In the step checker, type a line where you distribute −5 incorrectly. What does the checker say?

      It flags the line, because the new equation has a different solution. Every correct step keeps the same solution; that is what "equivalent" means.

      In the mixture balance, why is the mixture's salt the target percent of x + (the second amount), and not of x alone?

      The final mixture contains both solutions: x quarts plus the quarts of the second solution. The target percent applies to the whole mixture.

      What happens to the answer if the target percent is between the two percents? Outside them?

      Between them, x is positive. If the target is outside (for example 80% from 30% and 70% solutions), no positive amount works: you cannot make a mix stronger than both ingredients.
