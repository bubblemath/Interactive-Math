Math 1350 · Lesson 11 · scale drawings

Let the proportion
decide the direction.

A scale is the ratio of a length in the drawing to the matching length in reality. Your notes' 1 inch : 600 feet says one inch on the map stands for six hundred feet on the ground — and every question about that map is a proportion built from those two numbers.

✋

Same scale, opposite operations, and students apply whichever they did last. Going from the drawing outwards you multiply: 3 inches on the map is 3 × 600 = 1800 feet. Coming back the other way you divide: a real distance of 2400 feet is 2400 ÷ 600 = 4 inches on the map. Neither is hard, and choosing between them under pressure is where it goes wrong. Write it as a proportion and the direction stops being a decision — 1/600 = 3/x has only one solution, and you never have to recall which way round it went. Watch the units, too. 1 : 600 on its own is not the scale: the ratio is one inch to six hundred feet, and those are different units. Convert both to inches and the true scale factor is 1 : 7200 — a pure number that looks nothing like the one you started with.

How it works

Climb it, or jump to the rung you need

Rungs 1–3 read a scale and travel in both directions. Rung 4 is the direction trap and rung 5 removes it by writing a proportion. Rungs 6–7 convert inside the scale and recover a scale from measurements, and rungs 8–9 check plausibility and scale a whole shape.

Why it matters

Proportional reasoning you can see

The proportion removes the guesswork

Multiply or divide is a decision; a proportion is an equation. Writing one down converts a recalled rule into something you can solve.

Units live inside the ratio

1 : 24000 and 1 inch : 2000 feet are the same map scale. They look unrelated until both sides are put in one unit.

One factor for every length

Every measurement in the drawing shrinks by the same factor. That single fact is what keeps the shape recognisable.

A scale drawing is a proportion made visible — every length reduced by one shared factor. Write the scale as a ratio, set the known length against its match, and solve for the one you want; the equation knows which way to go even when you do not.
