Math 1350 · Lesson 12 · mental math with percents

Benchmarks,
then build.

Learn a handful of percents as fractions and the arithmetic disappears — 66⅔% of 90 is not a calculation, it is two-thirds of 90, which is 60. Everything else is built from 10% and 1% by halving and adding.

✋

The usual waste is computing the discount when you wanted the price. Your notes' Laura wonders whether $17 covers a $26.50 blouse at 40% off. Finding 40% and subtracting is two steps; noticing that she pays 60% is one — and 60% of $26.50 = $15.90, so she has enough. Whenever a question ends in “what do you pay”, take the complement straight away. And percents commute. x% of y and y% of x are both xy/100, so they are equal — which makes 18% of 50 awkward and 50% of 18 trivial, though they are the same question. Turning it round costs nothing and often saves everything.

Why it matters

The arithmetic a teacher does out loud

Fractions beat decimals here

A third is exact; 0.333 is not. Working from the fraction gives the right answer and gives it faster.

Ten and one build everything

Any whole percent is reachable from a tenth and a hundredth by halving and adding. No other tools are needed.

Answer the question asked

“Can I afford it?” wants the price, not the discount. Taking the complement first is one step instead of two.

Mental percent work is not a knack — it is a short list of fractions and two habits. Recognise the benchmark, build the rest from 10% and 1%, and if the question asks what you pay, take the complement before you start.
