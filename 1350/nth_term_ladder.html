One formula each —
and the mistake is always n − 1.

Arithmetic sequences have exactly one formula, aₙ = a₁ + d(n−1). Geometric sequences have exactly one, aₙ = a₁·r^(n−1). Almost nobody plugs into them wrong. What people get wrong is the (n − 1): the 10th term is nine steps from the first, not ten. Fix that one habit and every term you'll ever be asked for falls out.

to reach a₄ you take 3 steps — that's your n − 1

Count the steps, not the terms. Writing a₁ + d·n instead of a₁ + d(n−1) overshoots by exactly one d every single time. Same story in geometric: r^n instead of r^(n−1) multiplies by one extra r. This ladder baits that off-by-one on nearly every rung until reaching for (n − 1) becomes automatic.

Why it matters

The formula is the payoff of naming the type

It only works if you classified first

Plugging into aₙ = a₁ + d(n−1) is wasted effort if the sequence was geometric. This ladder assumes you can already tell them apart.

The far term is the whole point

Anyone can list to the 5th term. The formula exists so you can reach the 57th — or the 500th — without writing a single term in between.

Real problems run it backward

Theater rows and molecule counts don't ask "what's the nth term?" — they hand you the term and ask which one it is. Same formula, solved for n.

There's genuinely one formula per type here, and it is not the hard part. The hard part is a single subtraction hiding inside it. Count steps, not terms — the nth term is n − 1 steps from the first — and the arithmetic takes care of itself.
