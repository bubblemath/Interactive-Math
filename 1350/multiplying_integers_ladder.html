Math 1350 · Lesson 8 · multiplying integers

Two negatives make a positive.
Here is why.

Multiplying by a positive is repeated addition — put down that many groups. Multiplying by a negative is repeated subtraction — take that many groups away. Extend the definition that far and every sign rule follows; nothing has to be decreed.

the pattern has nowhere else to go
every row rises by 2 — and the pattern
does not stop just because the multiplier turned negative
travelling: speed × time
multiplier = speed · positive is east, negative is west
multiplicand = hours · positive is future, negative is past

−2 · −4 — moving west at 2 mph,
where were you 4 hours ago?
8 miles east. Going backwards, in reverse,
puts you forwards.
✋

Almost every student already “knows” that a negative times a negative is positive — and almost none can say why. That is the gap this ladder is for. “Two negatives cancel” is a mnemonic borrowed from grammar; “because that is the rule” restates the question. The real reason is that multiplying by −3 means removing three groups, and removing negatives from a pile worth nothing leaves positives behind. Throughout this lesson the first number is the multiplier: −3(2) means take away three groups of 2, not three groups of −2.

Why it matters

The rule everyone memorises and nobody justifies

Children will ask

“Why do two negatives make a positive?” is one of the most common questions in a middle-school classroom, and “because they just do” teaches that mathematics is arbitrary.

Patterns are proof enough here

A column whose answers climb by two, then simply keep climbing, is an argument a ten-year-old can follow and reconstruct.

Consistency is the deeper reason

Any other choice would break the distributive property. The sign rule is not a convention — it is forced by wanting arithmetic to keep working.

Multiplying by a negative is not a strange new operation; it is repeated subtraction, which you have been doing since Lesson 4. Put down groups when the multiplier is positive, take groups away when it is negative — and what is left when you remove negatives from nothing is exactly the positive answer the rule predicts.
