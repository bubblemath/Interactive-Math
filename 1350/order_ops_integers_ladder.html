The tiers are the same.
The minus sign isn't.

Nothing about the four tiers changed when negatives arrived. What changed is that the minus sign now has three jobs — negative, subtract, and the opposite of — and precedence decides which one it is doing. Exponents outrank negation, so in −3² the 3 gets squared before the minus ever gets a turn.

Exponents bind tighter than negation. In −3² the exponent reaches the 3 first, so you square and then negate: −(3 · 3) = −9. Only brackets can hand the minus sign to the base. Since you are the ones who will be writing this notation for children, the honest advice is not to rely on the reading at all — write −(3)² when you mean −9 and (−3)² when you mean 9, and the ambiguity never arises. This ladder drills all three forms so you can read them and so you know which two to prefer.

Why it matters

Notation you will have to write, not just read

It is the top single error

More marks are lost to −32 than to any other line in this topic, and almost always by students who could do every other part.

Brackets are free

Nothing is lost by writing −(3)2 or (−3)2. Clear notation is a kindness to the reader, and your readers will be children.

Odd powers hide the problem

A class drilled only on cubes will never see the distinction, then meet it cold on a square. Meeting both is the point.

The order of operations did not change when negatives arrived — but the minus sign became three symbols wearing one mark, and precedence is what tells them apart. Ask what the exponent is attached to before you do anything else, and when you write the expression yourself, use brackets so nobody has to ask.
