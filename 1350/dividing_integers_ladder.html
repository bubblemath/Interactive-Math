Math 1350 · Lesson 8 · dividing integers

No new sign rules.
Just the old ones, backwards.

a ÷ b = c means exactly one thing: b · c = a. So every division sign rule is a multiplication fact read from the other end — there is nothing here to memorise a second time. What is new is where division breaks, and it breaks in three different ways.

every division is a multiplication in reverse
find the missing factor, and the sign comes with it
like signs give a positive quotient · unlike signs give a negative one
✋

Division is the one operation that escapes the integers. Add, subtract or multiply any two integers and you land on an integer every time — but −7 ÷ 2 is not an integer at all, and no amount of sign-rule knowledge helps. That single failure is why the next lesson has to build a new number system. And the two zero cases fail for opposite reasons: −12 ÷ 0 has no answer, because nothing times 0 gives −12, while 0 ÷ 0 has every answer, so no unique one exists.

Why it matters

The operation that runs out of numbers

Inverses save memorising

Four multiplication sign rules and four division sign rules would be eight things to learn. Understanding the inverse makes it four.

Zero fails twice, differently

“Undefined” hides two distinct failures — no answers at all, and too many. Children who meet both remember why neither works.

This is why fractions exist

Integers are closed under three operations and not the fourth. Lesson 9 exists to repair exactly that gap.

Dividing integers asks nothing new of you: work out the size, then let the multiplication fact tell you the sign. The genuinely new content is where the operation fails — dividing by zero, and dividing when the answer simply is not an integer at all.
