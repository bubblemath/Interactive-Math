A proportion says two ratios are equal, and your notes define it exactly: a/b = c/d if and only if ad = bc. Solving one is easy. Writing one down correctly is not — because more than one arrangement is right, and the wrong ones look just like them.

✋

Two of these setups are correct and one is not, and nothing about the wrong one looks wrong. Your notes' glue-stick problem can be written 3/8 = x/120 or 3/x = 8/120 — both keep matching quantities in matching positions, and both give 45. But 3/8 = 120/x puts sticks over students on the left and students over sticks on the right, and produces 320. The rule is positional: whatever kind of quantity sits on top on one side must sit on top on the other. Label the positions before writing any numbers. And watch the second constant. A car at $40 a day gives C = 40D with k = 40 — but solving for days gives D = C/40 with k = 1/40. One situation, two constants of proportionality, reciprocal to each other, and which you get depends on which variable you chose to call y.

Why it matters

Where proportional reasoning actually fails

Setting up beats solving

Cross-multiplying is mechanical. Deciding what goes where is the part that needs thought, and it is where the marks are lost.

Proportional is stronger than linear

A constant rate is not enough — the relationship must also pass through the origin. A deposit plus a daily rate has one but not the other.

The constant has units

40 dollars per day and 1/40 days per dollar describe one arrangement. Reading the units tells you which constant you are holding.

A proportion is two ratios set equal, and the arithmetic that follows is the easy half. Decide what each position means before writing a single number — matching quantities on top, matching quantities underneath — and the cross products will take care of the rest.
