The trap is reaching for the algorithm. It always works, so it never forces you to look at the numbers \u2014 and looking at the numbers is the entire skill here. One honest caveat throughout this ladder: more than one strategy often works. 9 + 7 can be made into a ten or counted back from one. Each rung asks for the best fit for these particular numbers, and where a second strategy is also reasonable, the feedback says so.

Why it matters

Fluency is flexibility, not speed

Strategies build number sense

A child who turns 8 + 5 into 10 + 3 has learned something about how numbers come apart. A child who counts on fingers has learned only this one answer.

Estimation is a judgment

Knowing an answer should be "about 1,300" catches the errors an algorithm will happily produce. It is the habit that makes arithmetic self-checking.

Naming lets you teach it

You already do most of this in your head. Putting names to the moves is what lets you explain them to a child instead of just being fast.

The algorithm is a fallback that always works and never notices anything. Look at the numbers first \u2014 the reshaping they invite is usually visible before you write a single digit.
