Math 1350 · Lesson 9 · equivalence & simplifying

One law,
two directions.

The Fundamental Law of Fractions is the only tool in this ladder. Run it upwards and you build common denominators; run it downwards and you simplify. Every method your notes list — simplify both, use the LCM, use any common denominator, cross-multiply — is this one statement wearing a different hat.

the fundamental law of fractions
for any integer n ≠ 0
✋

The law says multiply or divide — it never says add or subtract. Taking 5 off the top and 5 off the bottom of 6/21 gives 1/16, which feels evenhanded and is nowhere near equal: 6/21 is about 0.29, and 1/16 is about 0.06. Multiplying scales both parts by the same factor, which keeps the ratio; subtracting changes the ratio every time. The second trap is stopping early. Dividing 12/42 by 2 gives 6/21 — a perfectly legal move that has not finished the job. Divide by the GCF instead, and one step lands you on 2/7.

Why it matters

The engine every other operation runs on

Adding needs it upwards

You cannot add thirds to quarters until both are twelfths, and building those twelfths is the law running up.

Answers need it downwards

Every product and quotient in the next four ladders finishes with a simplification, and the GCF is what makes that one step instead of several.

Lesson 7 comes back here

GCF simplifies, LCM builds common denominators. The whole of the previous lesson turns out to have been preparation for this one.

Equivalent fractions are not different numbers that happen to be close; they are the same number written with a different unit. Scale both parts by the same factor and nothing moves — which is why the law permits multiplying and dividing, and refuses adding and subtracting.
