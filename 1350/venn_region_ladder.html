"A" and "only A" are different pictures. Writing A shades the entire A circle — the overlap with B included — while "only A" is A ∩ B′, the crescent that avoids B. Two more quiet ones: complement A′ also fills the region outside both circles, and the third circle turns 4 regions into 8. Every rung keeps those live.

Why it matters

The picture is where survey problems get solved

Regions are the bridge to data

Every survey problem is a Venn diagram with numbers in the regions. If you can't shade "only A," you can't fill in "liked only comedies."

"Only" is a K–6 stumbling block

Children read "A" as "only A" constantly. Seeing the two regions side by side is the fix — and it's the same fix that works for adults.

Same words, second sense

You drilled ∪ ∩ ′ − on element lists; here they're pictures. Two ways of seeing the same idea makes it stick.

The letters tempt you to grab a whole circle when the region is just a sliver of it. Read the and/or/not, shade one slice at a time, and remember the world outside the circles is a region too.
