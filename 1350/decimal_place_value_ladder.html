The mirror is the
ones place.

The decimal point is not a separator and not a line of symmetry — it is punctuation. Place values run straight through it, dividing by ten at every step: …10², 10¹, 10⁰, 10⁻¹, 10⁻²… And decimal itself comes from the Latin decem, meaning ten, which is the only fact this whole system rests on.

where the reflection actually happens
tens pairs with tenths, hundreds with hundredths — across the ones
There is no “oneths” place — because 100 = 1 is its own reflection,
and it is already called ones.
✋

Almost everyone mirrors on the decimal point, and it is off by one place. Looking at hundreds · tens · ones . tenths · hundredths it is natural to fold the strip at the dot — but then ones would have to pair with tenths, and there would need to be a “oneths” place for the ones to pair with. There is not. The centre of the system is 100 = 1, sitting alone, and the pairs step outward from there. The second trap is 10−3: that minus sign means invert, not negate — it is 1/1000, a small positive number, not −1000. Exactly the same confusion as −x in Lesson 8.

Why it matters

One idea, extended in both directions

Place value is the whole system

Everything decimals can do — comparing, aligning, shifting by powers of ten — follows from knowing which place a digit sits in and what that place is worth.

The unit is a choice

Base-ten blocks have no fixed values, only fixed ratios. Declaring which piece is 1 is what lets the same apparatus teach whole numbers and decimals.

Reading aloud reveals understanding

A child who says “point four five six seven” may not know what any of those digits is worth. Naming the last place forces the question.

Decimal notation is one idea extended in both directions from a single centre. Find the ones place, count outward, and remember that each step right divides by ten — which is why the names pair up across the middle rather than across the dot.
