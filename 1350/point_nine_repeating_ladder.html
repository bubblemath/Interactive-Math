Two names,
one number.

Not nearly 1. Not approaching 1. Equal to 1 — the same number written a second way, exactly as 3/6 and 1/2 are the same number written two ways. Three different arguments all land there, and the difficulty is never the arithmetic. It is believing the arithmetic.

0.9‾ = 1
and the reason from your notes is the cleanest of all:
there is no number between them, so they cannot be different numbers
✋

The obstacle is not a calculation error — it is a belief about what the notation means. Students who can carry out every step of the algebra still refuse the conclusion, because they read 0.999… as a process that keeps going rather than a number that already is what it is. Something still travelling feels like it has not arrived. But 0.9 with a bar is not a journey and not a sequence — it is a single number, and that number is 1. So most of this ladder is spent on the objections rather than the proofs: “it is only approaching”, “there must be a tiny gap”, “they look different”. Each has a clean answer, and a future teacher will meet all three.

Why it matters

The question a class will actually ask

It will come up

Some child will notice that a third is 0.333… and ask what three thirds comes to. A teacher who has thought it through in advance answers well.

Notation is not the number

One number can have several names. 3/6, 1/2 and 0.5 are three; 0.4999… is a fourth. Looking different proves nothing.

Conviction is not the same as proof

Believing something firmly and being able to justify it are different skills. This ladder is about the second.

A number and its name are not the same thing, and one number may answer to several names. Between 0.9 repeating and 1 there is no room for anything at all — and two numbers with nothing between them are one number, written twice.
