Still one question: what changed?

Addition had four properties; multiplication has six. The two extras are exactly where students get lost — because 1 and 0 do completely different jobs, and only one of them is the identity.

Zero is not an identity. An identity leaves a number unchanged; zero does the opposite — it destroys whatever it touches. They are two separate properties, and calling 0 · 47 = 0 "the identity property" is the most common slip in this lesson. There is a second trap waiting from last lesson: 0 is the identity for addition, 1 for multiplication. A student fresh from Lesson 4 has the wrong answer already loaded.

Why it matters

Properties are permissions

They license every shortcut

Multiplying 4 · 25 first inside 4 · 25 · 7 is the associative property. Naming it tells a child their method is legitimate, not lucky.

Zero deserves its own name

That 0 · b = 0 is a property, not a fact to memorise, is what stops children from guessing when they meet 0 · 847.

Division shows the contrast

Multiplication has all six; division has none of the first three. Seeing what division lacks is what makes the properties feel like content.

Six named properties look like terminology and are really a list of moves you are allowed to make. Ask what changed — the order, the grouping, nothing at all, everything at once, the set you are in, or the operation itself.
