Math 1350 · Lesson 12 · percent change & reversals

The base
moves.

A percent change is a percent of where you started — and the result becomes a new starting point. Everything awkward about this topic follows from that one sentence: changes will not reverse, will not add, and cannot be undone by applying their opposite.

✋

Use multipliers and all three difficulties collapse into one idea. A 20% increase is × 1.2; a 20% decrease is × 0.8. Multipliers compose by multiplying, which is why percent changes do not add — your notes' Leo takes two 10% raises and gets 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21, a 21% rise, not 20%, because the second raise is of his already-raised salary. It is why up-then-down does not return you home: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, so you finish 4% below where you began, whatever the starting amount. And it is why undoing a change means dividing. Brittany's scooter sold for $1440 after 20% off, so $1440 ÷ 0.8 = $1800 — while adding 20% back gives $1728, which is wrong because that 20% is of the smaller number.

Why it matters

Where everyday percent reasoning goes wrong

Sales stack multiplicatively

“30% off, then a further 10% off” is 37% off, not 40%. Shops rely on the difference being invisible.

A cut and a raise are not inverse

A 10% pay cut followed by a 10% rise leaves you 1% down. The two percents are of different salaries.

Backwards means dividing

Removing tax from a total, or finding a pre-sale price, is a division — and applying the percent again is the standard mistake.

Percent changes compose the way multipliers do, because that is what they are. Turn every change into one number — up 20% is × 1.2, down 20% is × 0.8 — multiply them together to combine, and divide to undo.
