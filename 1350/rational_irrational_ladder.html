A pattern is not
a repeating block.

Your notes give three characteristics: infinitely many digits to the right of the point, no repeating block, and not expressible as a/b. The first two together are the third — Lesson 10 proved the fractions are exactly the terminating-and-repeating decimals, and this is that theorem turned around.

✋

You cannot settle this by looking at the digits. 1/17 begins 0.0588235294117647… and looks utterly patternless — but it repeats with a block of sixteen, and it is a fraction by construction. Meanwhile 0.1010010001… has an obvious visible pattern and is irrational, because the gaps keep growing so no fixed block ever cycles. A pattern is not a repeating block, and no finite stretch of digits decides anything. Nor does a radical sign. √9 = 3 and √0.25 = 0.5; for a whole number the test is whether the radicand is a perfect square, not whether a root is written. And 22/7 is not π — it is a famous approximation, it is rational, and its decimal repeats with period 6.

Why it matters

The number line has holes the fractions cannot fill

A length you can draw but not write

The diagonal of a one-by-one square exists, can be measured, and is not any fraction. That discovery is said to have shocked the people who made it.

Lesson 10 already did the work

Fractions are exactly the terminating-or-repeating decimals. Anything that neither stops nor cycles therefore cannot be a fraction — no new proof needed.

Appearance proves nothing

Sixteen unpredictable digits can still be a sixteenth of a cycle, and an obvious pattern can still fail to repeat. Structure decides; eyes do not.

Irrational numbers are not exotic — they outnumber the fractions, and one of them is the diagonal of the simplest square you can draw. Ask how the number was built, not what its digits look like: a ratio of whole numbers is rational, a root of a non-square is not, and a decimal that neither stops nor cycles cannot be a fraction at all.
