Math 1350 · Lesson 6 · order of operations

Four tiers.
Not six.

PEMDAS looks like a list of six things to do in order. It is really four — because multiplication and division share a tier, and so do addition and subtraction. Within a shared tier you work left to right, and that single detail is where nearly every wrong answer comes from.

what the mnemonic costs

left to right: 24÷6=4, then 4×28
“multiply before divide”: 6×2=12, then 24÷122
20 − 8 + 3
left to right: 20−8=12, then 12+315

✋

“Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally” is the problem. Six words in a row read as six steps in a row, so students do every multiplication before any division, and every addition before any subtraction. Both halves of that are wrong. Your worksheet already fixes it by drawing an arrow across the MD pair and across the AS pair rather than listing them separately — this ladder drills exactly what that arrow is trying to say.

Why it matters

A convention, not a law

It is an agreement

Nothing in arithmetic forces this order. It is a shared convention so that one written expression means one thing to everybody — worth telling children, because it answers “says who?”

Left to right is the tie-breaker

Within a tier the operations genuinely have equal standing, so something has to break the tie. Reading order does it, exactly as it does in a sentence.

The mnemonic needs repairing

If you teach PEMDAS at all, teach it as four tiers with two of them paired — otherwise you are installing the error yourself.

Multiplication and division are the same operation seen from two sides, and so are addition and subtraction — which is exactly why neither pair can outrank itself. Work down the four tiers, and inside a tier simply read left to right.
