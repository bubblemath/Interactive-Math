Twos and fives,
or it repeats.

A fraction is a division waiting to be carried out, and the decimal is what you get when you do it. Whether that division ever stops is not a matter of luck — it is decided entirely by the prime factors of the denominator, which makes this the second place Lesson 7 comes back to do real work.

the rule
A fraction in lowest terms terminates exactly when its denominator's
only prime factors are 2s and 5s — because 10 = 2 · 5, and nothing else divides a power of ten.
✋

“In lowest terms” is not decoration — it is the whole rule. Look at 9/24: the denominator factors as 2³ · 3, that 3 says “repeats”, and the answer is wrong. Reduce it first and you get 3/8, whose denominator is 2³ — so it terminates at 0.375. Anyone applying the test to the fraction as written will get this backwards, and written-form denominators are exactly what students are handed. Reduce, then factor, then decide.

Why it matters

Two notations, one set of numbers

Children meet both

Money is decimal, recipes are fractional, and measurement is either. Moving between them fluently is a daily requirement rather than an exam topic.

Repeating is not approximate

0.3̄ is exactly a third — not nearly. The bar is what makes the statement exact, and dropping it is what makes it false.

Every fraction is rational

Terminating or repeating, a fraction's decimal always does one or the other. A decimal that does neither is not a fraction at all — which is where irrationals begin.

Whether a decimal stops is a fact about primes, not about luck. Reduce the fraction, factor the denominator, and look for anything that is not a 2 or a 5 — if there is nothing else there, the division will end, and if there is, it never will.
