Subtraction is addition
wearing a disguise.

Once integers exist, subtraction stops being a separate operation with rules of its own. a − b is simply a + (−b) — every time, with no exceptions. Learn one conversion and every subtraction problem becomes an addition problem you already know how to do.

the same conversion, four ways
change the operation, flip the second number — both moves, every time
a − b = a + (−b)
✋

Subtracting a negative makes you richer, and every instinct says otherwise — 6 − (−2) = 8 is larger than the 6 you started with. Your worksheet's mail carrier settles it: taking away a bill is good news, and nobody argues with that. The second trap is doing half the conversion. “Add the opposite” is two moves — change the sign of the operation and flip the second number — and a student who does one gets 6 − (−2) = 4, which is arithmetically tidy and completely wrong.

Why it matters

One operation instead of two

Integers make subtraction total

In whole numbers 3 − 10 had no answer. Once opposites exist, every subtraction works — which is the real reason the integers were invented.

The rules stop multiplying

Rather than four sign rules for subtraction, there is one conversion and the addition rules you already have. Fewer rules, better remembered.

Order still matters

Addition is commutative and subtraction is not, and no amount of rewriting changes that — a − b and b − a are opposites, never equal unless both are the same.

Every subtraction in this lesson can be turned into an addition before you do anything else, and doing that first removes almost every chance of a sign error. Change the operation, flip the number that follows it, and then use the addition rules you already trust.
