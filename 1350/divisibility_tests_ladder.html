Math 1350 · Lesson 6 · divisibility tests

Don't divide.
Read the digits.

Ten tests, and not one of them asks you to divide. Each rule reads a different part of the numeral — the last digit, the last two, the last three, the sum of them all, or an alternating sum. Learn which part each test looks at and a five-digit number can be screened against all ten in about ten seconds.

each test reads a different part of 64,580
So 64,580 is divisible by 2, 4, 5 and 10 — and by nothing else on the list.
✋

The composite tests are where people fall. A number is divisible by 6 only if it passes both the 2 test and the 3 test — checking that it is even and stopping there is the single most common error. And 3 and 9 look like the same rule wearing different hats: both add the digits, but 9 is far stricter. 1,002 has digit sum 3, so it passes the 3 test and fails the 9 test. Anything divisible by 9 is automatically divisible by 3 — never the other way round.

How it works

Climb it, or jump to the rung you need

The rungs run in the order your worksheet teaches them — easiest first, not numerical order. Last-digit tests, then last-two and last-three, then the digit sums, then the composite and the awkward ones, and finally a full screen of one number against everything.

Why it matters

The gateway to factors, primes and fractions

Factoring starts here

Finding the factors of a number, reducing a fraction, hunting a common denominator — all of it begins with "what divides this?" answered quickly.

The rules have reasons

The 3 test works because every power of ten is one more than a multiple of three. Children who meet the reason remember the rule; children who only memorise it forget by June.

Screening is a real skill

Deciding a number is not divisible without doing the division is exactly the kind of judgment that makes arithmetic feel fast rather than laborious.

Every one of these tests is a shortcut past long division, and each one is looking at a different slice of the numeral. Ask which digits the rule reads — the last one, the last few, or all of them at once — and the answer is usually visible before you have finished writing the number down.
