The digits
never move.

Multiplying by 10ʳ raises every term's exponent by r. The digits keep their order and their spacing — what changes is which place each one sits in. “Move the decimal point” describes the result accurately and explains nothing, and this ladder is about the difference.

10² · 34.762 — watch the exponents, not the point
every term goes up by 2, and the digits 3 4 7 6 2 stay exactly as they were
3476.2 — the same five digits, two places further left
and dividing by 10² is the very same thing with r = −2
✋

Two signs point in opposite directions, and students cross them. Multiplying by 10² makes a number bigger, so the point appears to move right; dividing makes it smaller and the point appears to move left. The reversal happens because “negative exponent” feels like it ought to mean “go rightwards into the decimals” — but 10⁻² is 1/100, and multiplying by a hundredth shrinks things. Your notes give the fix by writing division as multiplication: 34.762 ÷ 10² = 34.762 · 10⁻². Once every shift is a multiplication, the sign of the exponent alone tells you the direction.

Why it matters

The machinery the rest of the lesson runs on

2ⁿ · 5ⁿ = 10ⁿ decides everything

Why only denominators of 2s and 5s terminate is not a separate fact — it is this identity, met here as exponent arithmetic before it is needed as a rule.

Shifting is how fractions convert

Turning 7/250 into 28/1000 is a deliberate shift into a power of ten, and it needs this rung's fluency.

A rule you can rebuild

Students who only know “move the point” cannot say which way when they are tired. Students who know the exponents changed can work it out every time.

Every decimal shift is a multiplication by a power of ten, and every power of ten changes the exponent of every term by the same amount. Write division as multiplication by a negative power, and the sign of the exponent tells you the direction without any need to remember a rule.
