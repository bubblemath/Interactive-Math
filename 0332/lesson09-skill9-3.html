Skill 9.3: Entering Formulas on a Calculator

Goal: type formulas so a calculator reads them correctly, and avoid rounding in the middle.

Compound interest keystrokes

Questions to explore

Compare the first two presets. Why does 12 + 8 ÷ 4 − 2 give 12?

The calculator divides first, and only 8 ÷ 4: 12 + 2 − 2 = 12. To divide the whole top by the whole bottom, put each in parentheses: (12 + 8) ÷ (4 − 2) = 10.

Why does (−)3^2 give −9?

The calculator follows the order of operations: the exponent comes before the negative, so it computes −(3²) = −9. Typing ((−)3)^2 makes −3 the base: 9.

Compare …^12 × 10 with …^(12 × 10). Which is the 10-year amount?

Only ^(12 × 10) raises to the 120th power. Without parentheses the calculator raises to the 12th power and then multiplies the result by 10, a very different number.

Turn on "round first." Why does a tiny rounding change make dollars of difference?

The rounded number gets multiplied by itself 120 times, so the small error grows each time. Keep every digit in the calculator and round only the final answer to the cent.
