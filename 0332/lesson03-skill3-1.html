Skill 3.1: Simplifying Fractions

Goal: write numbers as products of primes, simplify fractions to lowest terms, and convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions.

Questions to explore

Build a tree for 360, then for 90. Does the order you split in change the primes at the bottom?

No. However you split it, every whole number greater than 1 has exactly one set of prime factors. 360 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 3 · 3 · 5 no matter how you start.

In the cancel machine, try 12/24. Why can't you just cross out the 2s in "12" and "24"?

The 2 in 12 is a digit, not a factor. The tiles show 12 = 2 · 2 · 3 and 24 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 3. Canceling the matching primes leaves 1/2, not 1/4.

Try 90/39. Only one tile cancels. Is 30/13 in simplest form even though it is bigger than 1?

Yes. Simplest form only means no common factor is left; 30 = 2 · 3 · 5 and 13 is prime, so nothing else cancels. As a mixed number it is 2 4/13.

Why does the machine refuse to cancel a 2 with a 3?

Canceling means dividing the top and bottom by the same number. 2/2 = 1, but 2/3 is not 1, so the value would change.
