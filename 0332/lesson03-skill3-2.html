Skill 3.2: Multiplying Fractions

Goal: multiply fractions, whole numbers, and mixed numbers, canceling common factors before you multiply.

Area model and cross-cancel tool

The area model shows a product of two proper fractions as the overlap of two shadings. The cross-cancel tool works for any fractions, whole numbers, or mixed numbers: it breaks everything into primes so you can cancel before you multiply.

Questions to explore

In the area model, 2/3 · 3/4 shades 6 of 12 boxes. Why is the bottom of the answer 3 × 4?

One fraction cuts the square into 3 columns and the other into 4 rows, so there are 3 × 4 = 12 boxes. The overlap is 2 columns × 3 rows = 6 boxes, so the product is 6/12 = 1/2.

Is the product of two proper fractions bigger or smaller than each one?

Smaller. Taking 3/4 of 2/3 means taking part of a part. That is why the overlap is always inside both shadings.

Try 18/30 · 35/6 in the cross-cancel tool. Compare it with multiplying first.

Multiplying first gives 630/180, which you then have to simplify. Canceling the primes first leaves 7 on top and 2 on the bottom: 7/2. Same answer, much smaller numbers.

Try 2 1/2 · 4/5. Why does the tool rewrite 2 1/2 first?

A mixed number is a sum (2 + 1/2), and you cannot multiply part by part. As an improper fraction, 2 1/2 = 5/2, and 5/2 · 4/5 = 2.
