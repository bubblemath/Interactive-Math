Skill 4.3: Multiplying Decimals & Powers of 10

Goal: multiply decimals by counting decimal places, and multiply or divide by 10, 100, and 1,000 by moving the decimal point.

Decimal-place counter

Type two factors, or pick a preset. The counter multiplies them as whole numbers, counts the decimal places, and puts the point back. You do not line up the decimal points to multiply.

Powers-of-10 mover

Start with a number and press a button. Multiplying by 10, 100, or 1,000 moves the decimal point right; dividing moves it left. One place for each zero.

    Percent shortcut: to change a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100 (point moves 2 places right): 0.0825 → 8.25%. To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100: 6.5% → 0.065.

    Questions to explore

    Try 0.06 × 0.4. The whole-number product is 24, but the answer needs three decimal places. What do you do?

    Count three places from the right of 24 and fill in a zero on the left: 0.024. Zeros in front hold the places, just as filled-in zeros did for adding.

    Is the product of two decimals always smaller than both of them?

    Only when both are less than 1, like 2.4 × 0.3 = 0.72 (smaller than 2.4). In 1.05 × 3.2 = 3.36, the product is bigger than both factors. Multiplying by a number less than 1 makes things smaller; by a number greater than 1, bigger.

    Use the mover: start at 4.75, press × 100, then ÷ 100. Where do you end up?

    Back at 4.75. Multiplying and dividing by the same power of 10 undo each other, which is why decimal → percent → decimal always returns to where you started.

    1.05 × 3.2 gives 3.360 by counting places. Why do we write 3.36?

    A zero at the right end of the decimal part does not change the value: 3.360 = 3.36. With money, though, keep two places: $3.40, not $3.4.
