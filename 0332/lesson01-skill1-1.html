Skill 1.1: Sets of Real Numbers

Goal: classify any real number as natural, whole, integer, rational, or irrational, and list every set it belongs to.

Classify any number

Type a number: −12, 0, 2.75, −3/5, 0.333…, √16, √2 (or sqrt(2)), 4π (or 4pi).

Questions to explore

Where does 0 go? Why not in the Natural circle?

Natural numbers are the counting numbers 1, 2, 3, … and nobody starts counting at 0. Zero is the one number that is whole but not natural.

Sort √16 and √2. Why do two square roots land in different places?

√16 = 4 exactly, so it is a natural number. 16 is a perfect square; 2 is not, so √2 = 1.41421356… never ends and never repeats. Always simplify first.

Is every integer a whole number? Is every whole number an integer?

Every whole number is an integer (the Whole circle is inside the Integer circle). But not every integer is whole: −9 is an integer that sits outside the Whole circle.

Why is the Irrational box beside the Rational box instead of inside it?

A number cannot be both. Rational and irrational split the real numbers into two groups with no overlap. In Math 1332 you will call sets like these disjoint.
