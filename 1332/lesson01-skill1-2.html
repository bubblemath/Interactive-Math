Skill 1.2: Estimation

Goal: estimate sums, products and quotients with friendly numbers, and use estimates to check answers.

Estimate-then-check

    Questions to explore

    Estimate the lunch to the nearest dollar, then up to the next dollar. Which would you use to decide how much cash to bring?

    Nearest dollar gives $26, close to the exact $26.85. Rounding every price up gives $30, which is always at least the real total, so it is the safe amount to bring (before tax and tip).

    Try 248 + 797 with "first digit" and then "first two digits." Which estimate is closer? Which is easier?

    First digit: 200 + 800 = 1,000. First two digits: 250 + 800 = 1,050, closer to the exact 1,045. More digits make a closer estimate but harder mental math.

    For 897 ÷ 31, why does the estimate use 900 ÷ 30 instead of 900 ÷ 31?

    Friendly numbers for division should divide evenly. Round the divisor first (30), then pick a nearby number it divides: 900 ÷ 30 = 30.

    Try the "check" preset. How does the estimate catch the mistake?

    60 × 20 = 1,200, but the calculator said 12,230.46, about 10 times too big. An answer off by a factor of 10 usually means a decimal point was typed in the wrong place.
