Skill 4.2: Two-Set Survey Problems

Goal: fill a two-set Venn diagram from survey counts and use the union formula, including working backward.

Survey filler and working-backward solver

Type the counts a survey reports and fill the diagram one region at a time, starting with the people in both sets. Then use the union formula to find whichever number is missing.

Survey filler

    Working-backward solver

    Fill in four of the five boxes and leave one blank. The solver finds it from n(U) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B) + neither.

      Questions to explore

      In Example 1, why isn't region I equal to 810?

      The 810 Americans include the 290 Americans who agreed, and those 290 are in region II. Only 810 − 290 = 520 Americans are in region I.

      Try 810 + 1040 in Example 1. Why is it more than the people in A or G?

      810 + 1040 = 1850 counts the 290 people in both sets twice. Subtract them once: 1850 − 290 = 1560 = n(A ∪ G). The other 140 people are in region IV.

      Change n(A ∩ B) in the filler to something bigger than n(A). What happens?

      Region I would be negative, which is impossible: the overlap can't hold more people than A itself. The filler says the counts can't all be true.

      In the solver, leave n(U) blank instead. Does the formula still work?

      Yes. Any one of the five numbers can be found from the other four, because they are all linked by n(U) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B) + neither.
