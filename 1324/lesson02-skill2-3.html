Skill 2.3: Quadratic Regression with Desmos

Goal: decide whether data look linear or quadratic, fit a quadratic regression model, make predictions, and judge whether a prediction is interpolation or extrapolation.

Questions to explore

Turn on the straight line for the males data. Which model fits better, and how can you tell?

The quadratic. The points fall and then rise, and the line misses that turn. Its R² is much lower than the quadratic's, which is close to 1.

For the males data, predict x = 130 (the year 2030). Is the answer believable?

The model predicts about 104.6 males per 100 females, far above any value in the data. x = 130 is outside 60 to 110, so it is extrapolation. The U-shaped trend may not continue.

Pick the hours-studied data. Does a quadratic still help?

Barely. The a value is close to 0 and the R² for the line is almost as high. When data are close to a straight line, use a linear model.

Change one y-value a lot. How much does the curve move?

A single unusual point can pull the whole curve. Always look at the scatterplot before trusting a regression.
