One line, two costumes.

The same line can be written as Ax + By = C or as y = mx + b, and the two forms are not rivals — they are wearing different information on the outside. Standard form is already telling you the intercepts. Slope-intercept form is already telling you the slope. The whole skill of this lesson is knowing which costume you have on, and which one the question is asking for — because converting when you did not need to is where the fractions come from.

The engine of the whole lesson

What each form hands you for free

Take one line and write it both ways. Each form displays two of the four things you might want — and hides the other two behind a step of algebra.

These are the same line. Every point on one is a point on the other. But if the question wants the intercepts, the top row already has them and the bottom row makes you work — and if the question wants the slope, it is the other way around. Reaching for the form that already holds the answer is the entire strategy, and it is why your notes say intercepts are easiest from standard form.

✋ Don't convert unless the question makes you.

Converting 4x + 3y = 12 to slope-intercept form to find its intercepts is doing extra work to make the problem harder: it manufactures the fraction −43 and then asks you to compute with it. The intercepts were sitting right there in standard form. Match the form to what you want before you pick up the pencil.

Second: b carries its sign. In y = 2x − 6 the y-intercept is −6, not 6. The plus in the template y = mx + b is only correct half the time — read the actual sign in front of the constant, every time.

Third: in a word problem, the rate is m and the starting amount is b. A river receding at half a foot per day gives L = −0.5d + 34 — “receding” makes the rate negative. Writing +0.5 is a perfectly well-formed equation describing a flood.

Why this matters

Two forms, because two questions keep coming up

Slope-intercept is for graphing

Plot b, then step by the slope. The form is built for drawing, which is why every graphing task reaches for it and why reading m and b fluently is the whole first move.

Standard is for intercepts

Set one variable to zero and the other falls out in a single step. When a problem is about where a line meets the axes, this is the form that was waiting for you.

Word problems are already slope-intercept

A starting amount and a steady rate is exactly b and m. Once you see that, “write an equation” stops being a translation puzzle and becomes reading two numbers off a sentence.

There is only one line. The forms are two ways of writing it down, each keeping different information on the surface, and the reason there are two is that graphing and finding intercepts are two different questions that each have a form built to answer them. Choose the costume that already holds what you need, and most of this lesson is a single step of reading.
