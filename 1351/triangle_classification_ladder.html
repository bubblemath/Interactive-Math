Triangle Classification

Classify triangles by their angles and sides, test the triangle inequality, find missing angles using the 180° sum, and combine both classification systems.

Classify by Angles
NameAngle conditionDescription
Acuteall < 90°Every interior angle is less than 90°
Rightone = 90°Exactly one interior angle equals 90°
Obtuseone > 90°Exactly one interior angle is greater than 90°
Classify by Sides
NameSide conditionNote
Scaleneno equal sidesAll three sides have different lengths
Isosceles≥ 2 equal sidesAt least two sides are congruent; base angles are equal
Equilateral3 equal sidesAll sides equal; also equiangular (all angles = 60°)
Key Rules
RuleFormula / ConditionUse
Angle SumA + B + C = 180°Find a missing angle
Triangle Inequalitya + b > c (for each pair)Test if three lengths form a triangle
Equilateral ⇒ Equiangulareach angle = 60°All sides equal means all angles equal
Isosceles base anglesbase angles equalThe two angles opposite the equal sides are congruent
💡
Angle sum shortcut: Missing angle = 180° − (sum of the other two). A triangle can have at most one right or obtuse angle — the other two must always be acute.
⚠️
Triangle Inequality: Every pair of sides must sum to more than the third. If even one pair fails (a + b ≤ c), no triangle can be formed.
📐
Two systems, one triangle: Every triangle gets two labels — one by angles (acute/right/obtuse) and one by sides (scalene/isosceles/equilateral). For example: right isosceles, or acute scalene.
