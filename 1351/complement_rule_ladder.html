Complement
P(E′) = 1 − P(E)
Together they sum to 1
P(E) + P(E′) = 1
Work backward
P(E) = 1 − P(E′)

How it works

01 / Identify

Find P(E) first — from a fraction or by counting outcomes.

02 / Subtract

P(E′) = 1 − P(E). Subtract the fraction from 1.

03 / Simplify

Reduce the result to lowest terms before choosing.

04 / Streak

Get 4 in a row on a rung to earn its badge and advance.

⚠️

Common mistake: Students sometimes compute P(E′) = P(E) − 1 (subtracting in the wrong direction) or try to divide instead of subtract. The complement always uses subtraction from 1.

Why it matters

🔄

Easier route

Sometimes P(E′) is much easier to count than P(E). Find the complement and subtract — a classic problem-solving shortcut.

🎯

"At least one" problems

P(at least one success) = 1 − P(zero successes). The complement is usually far simpler to calculate.

📉

Risk analysis

If the probability a system fails is known, the complement gives the reliability — the probability it works.

🔢

Counting shortcuts

In combinatorics, counting what you don't want and subtracting from the total is often the fastest path to the answer.
