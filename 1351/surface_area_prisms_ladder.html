Ladder 23 — Surface Area of Prisms & Pyramids

Surface area is the total area of every face of a 3-D solid. Think of unfolding a box into a flat net — the area of that net is the surface area. We work with cubes, rectangular prisms, triangular prisms, and square pyramids here. Earn a badge on each rung by answering 4 in a row correctly.

Surface Area Formulas — Prisms & Pyramids
SolidTotal Surface AreaNotes
Cube SA = 6s² 6 identical square faces; s = edge length
Rectangular Prism SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh 3 pairs of opposite rectangles; l = length, w = width, h = height
Triangular Prism SA = 2(½bh) + 3 rect faces 2 triangular bases + 3 rectangular lateral faces; add all 5 faces
Right Square Pyramid SA = s² + 2sl Square base (s²) + 4 triangular faces (each ½ × s × l); l = slant height
Key Vocabulary
TermMeaning
Lateral areaSum of the areas of the side faces only (NOT the bases)
Total surface areaLateral area + area of both bases
NetA 2-D unfolding of a solid that shows every face flat
Slant height (l)Height of a triangular face measured along the face; NOT the same as the solid's vertical height
Edge (s)The length of one side of a square base or cube face
💡
Net trick. Mentally unfold the solid into a flat net. Each face appears exactly once. Count the faces, find the area of each type, add them all up.
⚠️
Slant height ≠ vertical height. In a pyramid or prism, the slant height (l) runs along a triangular face. The vertical height (H) runs straight up through the center. The formula uses slant height.
📐
Composite solids. When two solids share a face (e.g., a cylinder on a box), that shared face is interior — don't count it twice. Subtract it from both solids' totals.
