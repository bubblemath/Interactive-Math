A∪B

Can they happen at the same time? That's the whole question.

Ten rungs — first master mutually exclusive "or," then tackle the overlap correction. Earn a badge on each rung by getting 4 in a row.

✓ Mutually Exclusive (no overlap)
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
A and B cannot both occur.
They share no outcomes.
⚠ Not Mutually Exclusive (overlap)
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)
A and B can both occur.
Subtract the double-counted overlap.

How it works

01 / Classify

Ask: can event A and event B happen at the same time? If yes → overlap formula.

02 / Count

Find n(A), n(B), and — if needed — n(A∩B), the outcomes they share.

03 / Apply

ME: add. Not ME: add then subtract the overlap once.

04 / Streak

Get 4 in a row on a rung to earn its badge and advance.

⚠️

Most common mistake: Using P(A) + P(B) when there IS an overlap. Any outcome that belongs to both A and B gets counted twice in the raw sum — the − P(A∩B) term removes the duplicate exactly once.

Why it matters

🃏

Card & dice problems

Most intro probability problems combine events with "or." Knowing when to subtract the overlap is the key skill.

📊

Venn diagrams

The union formula is just the algebra behind a Venn diagram — adding both circles then removing the middle once.

🔬

Medical diagnostics

P(symptom A or symptom B) requires the overlap correction when symptoms can co-occur — a critical clinical calculation.

📋

Survey analysis

When respondents can check multiple boxes, simply adding percentages double-counts overlaps. The union formula fixes it.
