Ladder 19 — Tessellations & Interior Angles

A tessellation tiles the plane perfectly — no gaps, no overlaps. The key test: do the interior angles meeting at every vertex sum to exactly 360°? Learn the interior angle formula for regular polygons, identify which polygons tessellate alone, and reason about why some do and some don't. 💡
The 360° rule. A polygon can tile the plane by itself only if its interior angle evenly divides 360°. Triangle: 360÷60 = 6 ✓. Pentagon: 360÷108 = 3.33… ✗.
⚠️
Only three regular tessellations exist. Triangles, squares, and hexagons are the only regular polygons whose interior angles divide 360° evenly — all others fail the test.
📐
Sum of interior angles. Any n-gon has angle sum (n − 2) × 180°. For a regular polygon each angle is that sum divided by n. Memorise: triangle 60°, square 90°, hexagon 120°.
