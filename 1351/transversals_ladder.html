Transversals & Parallel Lines

Name the eight angle positions a transversal creates, identify pair relationships, apply congruence and supplementary rules, and solve algebraic problems.

Quick Reference — Transversal Angle Pairs
Pair nameRelationshipLocationWhen lines are parallel
Corresponding ≅ (congruent) Same side of transversal, one at each parallel Always congruent (Corresponding Angles Postulate)
Alternate Interior ≅ (congruent) Between the parallels, opposite sides of transversal Always congruent (Alternate Interior Angles Theorem)
Alternate Exterior ≅ (congruent) Outside the parallels, opposite sides of transversal Always congruent (Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem)
Co-Interior (Same-Side Interior) Supplementary Between the parallels, same side of transversal Always supplementary — sum = 180°
Vertical Angles ≅ (congruent) Opposite angles at the same intersection Always congruent (no parallel lines needed)
Linear Pair Supplementary Adjacent angles on a straight line at one intersection Always supplementary (no parallel lines needed)
💡
Alternate angles: Look for a Z-shape (or N-shape) in the figure — the two angles inside the Z are alternate interior angles. They're on opposite sides of the transversal and always congruent when lines are parallel.
⚠️
Congruent vs. supplementary: Corresponding, alternate interior, and alternate exterior angles are congruent. Co-interior (same-side interior) angles are supplementary (sum = 180°). Don't mix them up!
📐
Angle positions: The transversal creates 4 angles at each intersection — label them 1–4 (top) and 5–8 (bottom). Interior angles are 3, 4, 5, 6; exterior angles are 1, 2, 7, 8.
