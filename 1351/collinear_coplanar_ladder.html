Collinear, Coplanar & Intersections

Ten rungs from defining collinear and coplanar points, through the three intersection rules of geometry, to parallel and skew lines in 3-D space.

Quick Reference
ConceptDefinitionKey fact
Collinear points Points that all lie on the same line Any 2 points are always collinear; 3+ may or may not be
Coplanar points Points that all lie in the same plane Any 3 points are always coplanar; 4+ may or may not be
Line ∩ Line Two distinct lines intersect in exactly one point Unless they are parallel (no intersection) or the same line
Line ∩ Plane A line not parallel to a plane intersects it in one point If the line lies in the plane, every point is in common
Plane ∩ Plane Two distinct planes intersect in a line Unless they are parallel (no intersection)
Parallel lines Coplanar lines that never intersect Same plane, same direction, no common point
Skew lines Non-coplanar lines that never intersect Not parallel either — they exist in different planes
💡
Always coplanar / collinear: Any 2 points are always collinear. Any 3 points are always coplanar. It takes at least 3 points to test collinearity, and at least 4 to test coplanarity.
⚠️
Skew ≠ Parallel. Skew lines do not intersect AND are not parallel — they live in completely different planes. Parallel lines are coplanar; skew lines are not.
📌
Plane ∩ Plane = Line. When two planes meet, their intersection is always a full line — never just a point.
