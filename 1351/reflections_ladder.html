Ladder 17 — Reflections

Flip every point across a mirror line — the x-axis, y-axis, y = x, or y = −x. Master the four coordinate rules, apply them to triangles, find the mirror line from a point and its image, and reason about what reflections preserve (and what they reverse). Earn a badge on each rung by answering 4 in a row correctly.

Reflection Coordinate Rules
Mirror LineRule: (x, y) →What changes
x-axis (y = 0)(x, −y)y-coordinate negated
y-axis (x = 0)(−x, y)x-coordinate negated
y = x(y, x)coordinates swap
y = −x(−y, −x)coordinates swap and both negate
Key Properties of Reflections
PropertyDetail
Rigid motion (isometry)Lengths, angles, perimeter, and area are all preserved
Orientation reversedClockwise pre-image → counter-clockwise image (unlike rotations/translations)
Perpendicular bisectorThe mirror line ℓ is the perpendicular bisector of every segment PP′
Distance to mirrorEach point and its image are equidistant from the mirror line
💡
Fold test. Fold your paper along the mirror line — the pre-image and image should land exactly on top of each other. This is the physical meaning of reflection.
⚠️
y = x vs y-axis. Reflecting over y = x swaps coordinates: (3, 1) → (1, 3). Reflecting over the y-axis negates x: (3, 1) → (−3, 1). These are easy to mix up.
📐
Orientation reversal. Every reflection reverses orientation (handedness). Two reflections in a row restore it — that composition is a rotation or translation.
