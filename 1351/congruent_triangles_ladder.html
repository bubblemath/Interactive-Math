Congruent Triangles

Master all five congruence shortcuts — SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and HL — learn why SSA and AAA are not valid, name congruence statements in the correct vertex order, and identify missing parts from CPCTC.

Congruence Postulates & Theorems
NameWhat you needNotes
SSS Side · Side · Side All three pairs of sides are congruent
SAS Side · Angle · Side Two sides and the included angle (between those sides)
ASA Angle · Side · Angle Two angles and the included side (between those angles)
AAS Angle · Angle · Side Two angles and a non-included side
HL Hypotenuse · Leg Right triangles only — hypotenuse and one leg
Not Valid Shortcuts
NameWhy it fails
SSA Side-Side-Angle — two triangles can match this pattern without being congruent (the "ambiguous case")
AAA Angle-Angle-Angle — proves similar, not congruent; sides can scale to any size
Key Vocabulary
TermMeaning
≅Congruent — same shape and same size
CPCTCCorresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent
Included angleAngle formed by the two sides being compared
Included sideSide between the two angles being compared
Congruence statement△ABC ≅ △DEF — vertices must match in the order of corresponding pairs
💡
SAS vs. SSA: The angle in SAS must be between the two sides. If the angle is not between them, it is SSA — not a valid shortcut.
⚠️
Vertex order matters: △ABC ≅ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. If vertices are listed out of order, the congruence statement is wrong even if the triangles are congruent.
📐
HL is right-triangles only: You can use Hypotenuse-Leg only when you already know both triangles are right triangles. For all other triangles use SSS, SAS, ASA, or AAS.
