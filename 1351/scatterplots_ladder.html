Scatterplots & Correlation

Ten rungs from reading scatterplots and identifying direction and form, to interpreting the correlation coefficient r and avoiding causation claims.

r = +1
Perfect Positive
Points fall exactly on an upward line
r ≈ 0
No Correlation
No linear pattern; cloud of points
r = −1
Perfect Negative
Points fall exactly on a downward line
|r|
Strength
Closer to 1 → stronger linear association
sign(r)
Direction
Positive → up; Negative → down
≠ →
Not Causation
Correlation does not imply causation
💡
Strength of r: |r| ≥ 0.8 → strong; 0.5 ≤ |r| < 0.8 → moderate; |r| < 0.5 → weak. These cut-offs vary by discipline — context matters.
⚠️
r is for linear patterns only. A curved (non-linear) association can be strong but have r ≈ 0. Always look at the plot, not just the number.
📌
Outliers matter: A single extreme point can dramatically pull r toward 0 or away from 0. Inspect your scatterplot before trusting r.
