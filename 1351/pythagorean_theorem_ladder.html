The Pythagorean Theorem

Apply a² + b² = c² to find missing sides, identify Pythagorean triples, solve real-world distance problems, and classify triangles by their sides.

The Theorem
FormulaUse
a² + b² = c²c is always the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle)
Find hypotenusec = √(a² + b²)
Find a lega = √(c² − b²)
ConverseIf a² + b² = c², then the triangle is a right triangle

Triangle Classification by Sides
If …Then the triangle is …
a² + b² = c²Right
a² + b² > c²Acute
a² + b² < c²Obtuse
💡
Hypotenuse is always c: Label the longest side (opposite the right angle) as c before substituting. The two shorter sides are the legs — order of a and b doesn't matter.
⚠️
Finding a leg, not the hypotenuse: Use a² = c² − b² (subtract, don't add). Mixing up addition and subtraction is the most common error.
📐
Converse check: Compare a² + b² with c² (where c is the longest side). Equal → right; greater → acute; less → obtuse.
