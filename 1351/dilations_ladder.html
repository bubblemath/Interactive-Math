Ladder 18 — Dilations & Scale Factor

Stretch or shrink every point away from a center of dilation by a scale factor k. Master the coordinate rule, classify enlargements and reductions, dilate single points and triangles, find the scale factor from a figure and its image, and reason about what dilations preserve — and what they change. Earn a badge on each rung by answering 4 in a row correctly.

Dilation Rules
ConceptRule / Detail
Coordinate rule (center = origin)(x, y) → (kx, ky) where k = scale factor
Enlargement|k| > 1 — image is larger than pre-image
Reduction0 < |k| < 1 — image is smaller than pre-image
Congruent copyk = 1 — image is identical to pre-image
Scale factor from lengthsk = image length ÷ pre-image length
Negative kDilation + 180° rotation about the center
What Dilations Preserve vs. Change
Preserved ✓Changed ✗
Angle measuresSide lengths (scaled by |k|)
Shape (similarity)Perimeter (scaled by |k|)
Parallel linesArea (scaled by k²)
Orientation (if k > 0)Distance from center to each point
💡
Area scales as k². If the scale factor is 3, each side is 3× longer but the area is 9× larger. If k = ½, area becomes ¼ of the original.
⚠️
Not a rigid motion. Dilations are similarities, not isometries — they do NOT preserve lengths or area. Only angles (and shape) stay the same.
📐
Center matters. The rule (x, y) → (kx, ky) only works when the center is the origin. If the center is some other point C, translate C to the origin first, dilate, then translate back.
