Symmetry

Identify and apply the three types of symmetry — line (reflectional), rotational (turn), and point — for common geometric figures and real-world shapes.

Three Types of Symmetry
TypeDefinitionTest
Line / Reflectional A figure has line symmetry if it can be folded along a line so both halves match exactly Count how many fold lines produce matching halves
Rotational / Turn A figure has rotational symmetry if it can be rotated between 0° and 360° and look identical to the original Angle of rotation = 360° ÷ n (where n = order / number of turns)
Point A special case of rotational symmetry — the figure maps onto itself by a 180° rotation about a center point Another name: central symmetry. A circle is the perfect example.

💡
Rotation formula: A regular n-gon has rotational symmetry of order n. The smallest angle of rotation is 360° ÷ n. A square (n=4): 360°÷4 = 90°.
⚠️
Point symmetry ≠ line symmetry: A parallelogram has point symmetry (180° turn) but ZERO lines of symmetry. Don't confuse the two types.
📐
Order of rotation: We don't count the full 360° turn — every figure maps to itself at 360°. Rotational symmetry requires a turn strictly between 0° and 360° that works.
