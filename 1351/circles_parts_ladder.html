Circles: Parts, Central Angles & Arc Measures

Name and locate every part of a circle, apply the Central Angle Theorem, and find arc measures for minor arcs, major arcs, and semicircles.

Parts of a Circle — Diagram
Circle Parts — Definitions
TermDefinition
CenterThe fixed point equidistant from every point on the circle
RadiusA segment from the center to any point on the circle; r
DiameterA chord through the center; d = 2r (the longest chord)
ChordA segment with both endpoints on the circle (not necessarily through center)
SecantA line that intersects the circle in exactly two points (an extended chord)
TangentA line that touches the circle in exactly one point (the point of tangency)
ArcA portion of the circle between two points on it
Minor arcAn arc shorter than a semicircle; its measure < 180°
Major arcAn arc longer than a semicircle; its measure > 180°
SemicircleExactly half the circle; its measure = 180°
Central Angle & Arc Relationships
ConceptRule
Central Angle An angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle; its two sides are radii
Central Angle Theorem Arc measure = Central angle measure. If the central angle is 70°, the intercepted arc is 70°.
Full circle Total arc = 360°. All central angles around a center sum to 360°.
Major arc measure Major arc = 360° − minor arc
Semicircle arc A diameter creates two semicircles, each = 180°
Naming arcs Minor arc: two letters (arc AB). Major arc / semicircle: three letters (arc AXB) to avoid ambiguity.
💡
Central Angle = Arc: A central angle of n° intercepts an arc of exactly n°. This is the most-used circle theorem at this level.
⚠️
Chord ≠ Diameter: A diameter is a special chord through the center. Every diameter is a chord, but not every chord is a diameter.
📐
Arc naming: Minor arc = 2 letters. Major arc or semicircle = 3 letters (middle letter is a point between the endpoints to clarify which arc you mean).
