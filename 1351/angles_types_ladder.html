Angles: Types, Complements & Supplements

Name angles by their measure, find complements and supplements, identify vertical angles, and solve algebraic angle problems.

Quick Reference — Angle Types & Pairs
NameMeasureFigureKey fact
Acute 0° < m∠ < 90° Less than a right angle
Right m∠ = 90° Exactly 90° — the little square marks it
Obtuse 90° < m∠ < 180° More than right, less than straight
Straight m∠ = 180° Two rays in exactly opposite directions
Complementary m∠A + m∠B = 90° Angles need not be adjacent
Supplementary m∠A + m∠B = 180° Angles need not be adjacent
Vertical m∠A = m∠C; m∠B = m∠D Opposite angles when two lines cross — always congruent
💡
Memory hook: Complementary → Corner (90°). Supplementary → Straight (180°). Complementary angles together make a right angle; supplementary angles together make a straight line.
⚠️
Adjacent ≠ required: Two angles are complementary or supplementary based only on their sum — they do not need to share a side or a vertex.
📐
Vertical angles: Formed by two intersecting lines. The two pairs of opposite angles are congruent — not supplementary. Adjacent pairs at the intersection are supplementary.
