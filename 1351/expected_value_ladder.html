E(X)

Find the long-run average of any random variable.

Ten rungs — from computing E(X) directly from a table, to variance and standard deviation, to real-world applications like games, insurance, and making decisions under uncertainty. Earn a badge with 4 correct in a row.

Expected Value
E(X) = Σ x · P(x)
Multiply each value by its probability
and add the products.
Variance & SD
σ² = Σ (x − μ)² · P(x)
σ = √σ²
Spread of the distribution

Fair Game / Decision
Fair game: E(X) = 0
Favorable: E(X) > 0
Positive E(X) → player gains on average.
Negative E(X) → house gains.

Key ideas

E(X) = weighted average

The expected value is not the most likely outcome — it is the long-run average if you repeated the experiment many times. It may not even be a possible value of X.

Net gain vs. gross gain

For games and lotteries, assign the correct signed value. A $10 prize on a $2 ticket is a net gain of $8, not $10. Losses are negative.

Variance measures spread

σ² = Σ(x − μ)² · P(x). Each squared deviation from the mean is weighted by its probability. Take the square root for standard deviation.

Decision rule

Choose the option with the higher E(X) when values are monetary. A fair game has E(X) = 0. Insurance has negative E(X) for the buyer — you pay for risk reduction, not expected profit.

💡
Sign discipline: Label each outcome with its net monetary result before computing E(X). If you pay $5 to play and win $20, the net value of that outcome is +$15, not +$20.
⚠️
Common mistake: Adding probabilities instead of multiplying. E(X) = Σ x · P(x), not Σ x + Σ P(x). Each x is multiplied by its own probability before summing.
